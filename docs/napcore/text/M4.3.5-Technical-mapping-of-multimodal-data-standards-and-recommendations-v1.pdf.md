Abstract
This milestone focuses on the technical mapping of parking data standards. To achieve this, the
existing NeTEx parking national profiles currently used in European countries (France and Italy) were
analysed.
Additionally, the report provides a technical mapping of the elements from various standards used in
the parking domain, specifically the NeTEx national parking profiles and APDS.
The document concludes with a series of recommendations and next steps to enrich the parking
standards.

1. Introduction
Parking data domain attracts a large interest from a variety of stakeholders from the road and public
transport fields. This offers a large and different range of points of view and use cases. In the scope
of the NAPCORE SWG 4.3 (multimodal standard) parking is observed as an important tool to support
the modal shift by attracting car users to take public transport. This vision was supported by the
European Commission in the publication of Regulation (EU) 2024/490 (MMTIS DR) amending
Regulation (EU) 2017/1926 as it brings the relevant parking data into the multimodal domain (“To be
consistent and avoid overlaps with Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/670 [RTTI DR],
specific data types on parking should be added to the scope of Delegated Regulation (EU)
2017/1926)”) 1.
In order to prove compatibility and interoperability between standards, a technical mapping is
needed. It is under the scope of the NAPCORE multimodal standardisation subworking group to
analyse the existing data standards under parking scope and deliver a technical mapping to support
interoperability and recommendations. The possible use of converters and/or validators in the scope
of DR (EU) 2024/490 is collected in several of its articles which can be summarised as: "Regarding the
exchange of static, historic, observed and dynamic travel and traffic data, data holders should make
that data accessible via the national access point as specified in Article 3 by using the standards and
technical specifications referred to in Articles 4 and 5. This can also be done under any digital
machine-readable format where data holders can prove that it is fully compatible with the reference
standards, including for example through automatic converters or validators. Hence a technical
mapping is necessary to prove the compatibility and interoperability of such convertors.

2. Parking data standards state-of-the-art
Parking data is an important element that facilitates the transition between transport modes (either
personal and/or public transport) and supports the modal shift from private means of transport
towards public transport. The fourth recital of the amended version of the MMTIS Delegated
Regulation (DR EU 2024/4902) concludes: “specific data types on parking should be added to the
scope of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/1926 [ …]. Data on parking, such as location and availability
of parking places, where and how to pay for parking, and information on parking tariffs, are
considered important for the further development of reliable travel information services, and should
be made accessible by data holders, such as transport authorities, transport operators,
infrastructure managers, transport on demand service providers or parking operators”.
In the European landscape, parking data can be found under different standards, the most common
being:
 Transmodel (EN 12896):
o NeTEx (CEN TS 16614) for static data
o SIRI (CEN TS 15531) for dynamic data
 APDS (ISO TS 5206-1:2022); which is linked to DATEX II (CEN TS 16157-6:2022).
Different initiatives aiming to bridge the gap between the different standards took place in the last
years. The most relevant of them being the HARP initiative (2018-2020) which included a first
mapping between NeTEx, DATEX II and APDS supported by the Transmodel ecosystem, DATEX II and
the European Parking Association. The work was initiated but it was not concluded due to the
rejection of the road counterparts during the DATEX II part 6 revision.
A few months after (end of 2021) a national NeTEx parking profile3 was finalised by the French public
transport standardisation committee which involved the participation of French parking
stakeholders. As a result, parking datasets are available in the French NAP in NeTEx format since
November 2024: https://transport.data.gouv.fr/datasets/indigo-open-data
More recently, during the celebration of the Mobility Data Days 2024 in Turin, Italian NAPCORE
partners reported that static parking data is being collected and published through the Italian NAP
following the Italian NeTEx parking profile 4. Moreover, dynamic parking data in SIRI format is
published nowadays in the regional access point of South Tyrol5.

3. National profiles and full NeTEx standard
A national profile can be understood as a subset of entities taken from the full standard. This subset
is identified as a way to help stakeholders implementing parts of the standard (NeTEx in this case)
avoiding work with the complete list of entities.
Figure 1 shows a draft of how the NeTEx profiles are part of the full NeTEx standard but taking parts
of it.
Figure 1: Connection between NeTEx and national profiles
In the figure 2 it is visualised the relation between national profiles and the minimum European
profile. Each country can choose to extend or limit its national profile as considered to cover the
national needs. As long as the minimum European profile is respected by the national profiles a
degree of interoperability should be guaranteed between the different datasets.

4. Comparison of existing NeTEx parking national profiles (France and
Italy)
A comparison of the available NeTEx national profiles (French and Italian) was done in order to
understand better their similarities and differences. A complete version of this comparison can be
found in the annex 1.
In the scope of NeTEx standard (TS-16614-1: Public transport network topology exchange format):
“NeTEx includes a model to describe parking elements. A PARKING is a type of SITE that describes
the availability of parking for different types of vehicles and its relation to other SITEs such as
stations.”.
The French NeTEx parking profile was downloaded from the French National Access Point. For this
mapping it was used the version 2.38 published on the 21st of November 2024:
https://normes.transport.data.gouv.fr/normes/netex/parkings/

Following the previous analysis the different types of vehicles authorised in the parking
(ParkingVehicleType enumeration) were compared. This enumeration is of importance as it
stablishes which types of vehicles can access the parking. Table 9 shows the comparison between
French and Italian profile and the existing gaps between them. It can be seen that some modes of
transport appear with different wording under French and Italian profile. This is the case for a
motorcycle; that in the Italian profile is motorcycle, but under the French profile appears as
motorbike. Similarly, a bicycle can be defined in the French profile as pedalCycle, while in the Italian
profile we find pedalCycle, cycle and eCycle. It has to be noted, that the French national parking
profile has undefined as one of the options to be selected. In that case, further determination of the
vehicle can be done with the vehicleTypes attribute.
For interoperability purposes, it should be considered that future versions of the existing NeTEx
national parking profiles and new national profiles take into consideration the existing enumerations
and use the same wording.

It should be specifically noted how the payment process is described in the French national NeTEx
parking profile, with elements such as parkingPaymentProcess, PaymentMethods, currencies or
cardsAccepted. Alternatively, the Italian profile these elements are not present. To fully explain its
approach, it has to be considered that in NeTEx a travel or parking ticket is described in several
steps, from the “atomic” access rights to the “logical” fare product and to the “physical” ticket
materialized on a physical support (paper, card or mobile application). The price can be attached to
each level.
For the NeTEx Italian profile an intermediate step (FareTable) was chosen and specifying discounts in
the physical level (SalesOfferPackage).
Hence, in the case of parking titles:
 ParkingTariff is used only to connect parking access rights (in terms of TimeIntervals) to the
Parking area
 The “logical tariff” (FareProduct) recalls the ParkingTariff
 The first level where prices are introduced is the FareTable
 The “physical level” SalesOfferPackage recalls:
o The FareProduct (and indirectly the ParkingTariff)
o The FareTable
o And applies discounts.
In this case, the final price can be calculated gathering all the information: the ParkingTariff alone is
not sufficient and has no price attached directly.

6. Gaps between standards
This section looks into the gaps found between the studied standards (NeTEx and APDS). Although
the scope of APDS and the studied NeTEx national profiles are the same (parking) there are
differences in the way data is organised.
Starting for a high level, it seems clear that both standards cover a similar scope (parking) as well as
related facets (geometry, distribution, bay availability, fares, payment methods, etc.).
Nonetheless, a deeper analysis and comparison of both standards show that some attributes cannot
be directly mapped (there is no correspondence for some entities in the source model towards the
target model). For example, the NeTEx entity ParkingType, which offers an enumeration list to
describe the very nature of the parking (e.g. parkAndRide, roadSideParking, airportParking, etc.).
Similar enumerations to this one can be found under Place.OperatingRestrictionEnum although it is
not exactly the same.
Similarly, ParkingLayout (which defines the very nature of the parking and the spatial structure)
cannot be traced to one specific attribute in APDS. Instead, similar enumerations were found spread
across three different attributes: Place.CoveredEnum; Place.topLevelNotCovered and
Place.StructureTypeEnum.
Similarly, there were attributes that could not be mapped to APDS. BayGeometry, which stands for
the distribution that cars are parked in the parking. ParkingVisibility, which determines the marking
of the parking is done or ParkingStayList, which lists the type of parking duration (e.g., midterm,
longTerm, dropoff, etc.).

7. Conclusions and Future Work
The present report showed a comparison between the French and Italian NeTEx parking profile and
a mapping between French and Italian NeTEx parking profile and APDS.
As found in during the technical mapping, there is a need for a more harmonised structure of the
standard documents is needed for the NeTEx profiles. Possibly, developing a template for profiles
will be of help in the future development of new parking profiles to ensure alignment between
member states.
Additionally, further alignment between the NeTEx national parking profiles would be useful. For
example, enumerations for some vehicles are different in French and Italian profile (e.g. bicycles).
In future iterations of this mapping, it would be useful to compare the enumerations of NeTEx and
APDS. For example, ParkingType in NeTEx FR includes certain types that are not covered by APDS
and vice versa (e.g., APDS includes ‘snow’ as one of the enumerations). This would require an even
more detailed analysis on the correspondences.
Future work on this technical mapping and conversion between NeTEx and APDS should be
continued in future stages. This continuation should take place in the future project NAPCORE-X as it
is the natural continuation of the NAPCORE project.
Regarding the future work on the technical mapping and conversion between NeTEx and APDS
should consider the following points:
First, the development of an EU NeTEx parking profile would be useful as well to align national
profiles.
Also, a deeper understanding of the enumerations and their respective mapping is necessary.
A description of the APDS enumerations could not be found in the Information Model document.
Without this description, the correct understanding of the enumerations can only be guessed at
best.
The continuation of the mapping work. This continuation can be extended to other NeTEx profiles
that are related to parking, but not covered by the current work (e.g., inherited elements, etc.).
Finally, gaps found between both standards have to be evaluated and can be filled if necessary to
secure alignment.
