Abstract
This is the first report of the cycling data ambassadors. Through a series of ‘Reports from the cycling
data ambassadors’ the cycling data ambassadors will regularly give updates about developments in
the domain of cycling and cycling infrastructure data that are relevant from the NAPCORE
perspective. The topics can vary per report, but in general will deal with topics as:
- Cycling data types in the Delegated Regulations
- Standards for cycling and cycling infrastructure data
- Availability of cycling and cycling infrastructure data in NAPs

1. Cycling and cycling infrastructure data standardisation
There is a need to harmonise current national standards for cycling and cycling infrastructure data,
and to come to European standards. For bike sharing already standards exist, but for cycling and
cycling infrastructure data this is not yet the case. Therefore a group of experts from the public and
private sector have joint efforts:
 to identify various standardisation initiatives on cycling data in various EU countries and to
identify overlaps and gaps in existing standards regarding cycling related data categories. For
example: cycling infrastructure is currently not covered by a CEN standard.
 to carry out a thorough requirements analysis with respect to cycling and cycling infrastructure
data.
 to collect relevant use cases, i.e. public and/or private sector driven use cases where
standardised cycling and cycling infrastructure data would be of great added value.
WG4 will assess which standards are most suitable to support the gaps. This action requires the input
from both the private sector and the public sector.
The first workshop on this topic was held on 18 April 2023. The presentations and a short report of
the workshop can be found here. [create link to minutes here] Other workshops are foreseen in 2023
and 2024. More information can be found in the News & Events section of our website.

2. The current status of cycling data in the delegated regulations
Two delegated regulations supplementing the ITS Directive (2010/40/EU) are relevant for cycling and
cycling infrastructure data in NAPs:
 the delegated regulation (EU) 2017/1926 on the provision of EU-wide multimodal travel
information services (in short: MMTIS), as well as
 the Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/670 on the provision of EU-wide real-time traffic
information services (in short: RTTI)
However, it should be noted that only certain data categories mentioned in these delegated
regulations and its annexes are relevant in the context of cycling and cycling infrastructure data:
 for Multimodal Travel Information Services (MMTIS) the following specific cycling data
categories are mentioned:
o Static traffic data:
i. Cycle network (segregated cycle lanes, on-road shared with vehicles, on-path
shared with pedestrians),
ii. How to book car sharing, taxis, cycle hire etc. (incl. retail channels, fulfilment
methods, payment methods)
iii. Detailed cycle network attributes (surface quality, side-by-side cycling,
shared surface, on/off road, scenic route, ‘walk only’, turn or access
restrictions (e.g. against flow of traffic)
iv. Bike sharing stations
v. Secure bike parking (such as locked bike garages)
o Types of dynamic travel and traffic data:
i. Cycling network closures/diversions
ii. Bike sharing availability
 for Real Time Traffic Information Services (RTTI) no specific cycling data categories are
mentioned, but in general data on road infrastructure, regulations and restrictions are
mentioned, which can also relate to cycling infrastructure and regulation/restrictions for cycling:
o The types of data on infrastructure:
 Road network links and their physical attributes, such as geometry, road
width, number of lanes, gradients, junctions
o The crucial types of data on regulations and restrictions:
 static and dynamic traffic regulations, where applicable:
a) access conditions for tunnels
b) access conditions for bridges
c) permanent access restrictions
d) one-way streets

o Other types of data on regulations and restrictions:
 the location and identification of traffic signs reflecting traffic regulations
and identifying dangers:
a) access conditions for tunnels
b) access conditions for bridges
c) permanent access restrictions
d) other traffic signs reflecting traffic regulations
o The crucial types of data on the state of the network:
a) road closures
b) lane closures
c) roadworks
d) temporary traffic management measures
o Other types of data on the state of the network:
a) bridge closures
b) accidents and incidents
c) poor road conditions
o The types of data on the real-time use of the network:
a) traffic volume
b) traffic speed
c) location and length of traffic queues
d) travel times
In summary one can conclude that there are mainly three categories of cycling data:
1) data on the cycling network, such as the network, physical attributes, access restrictions,
road closures, speed, volume, etc.
2) data on bike sharing and bike hire, such as bike sharing location, bike hire location,
availability, booking, etc.
3) data on secure bike parking (location, availability)
It should be noted that these specifications should not oblige any stakeholder to start collecting any
data that they are not already collecting or to digitise any data that is not already available in a digital
machine-readable format. The specific requirements only apply to the data that is actually collected
and available in a digital machine-readable format.

3. NeTEx extension for new modes, including bike sharing
Note: the following text on NeTEx is (partly) based on information from https://netexcen.eu/?page_id=534
NeTEx (Network Timetable Exchange) defines a standard for exchanging public transport passenger
information data in XML format. The functional scope of NeTEx is divided into five parts, each
covering a functional subset of the CEN Transmodel conceptual model for Public Transport
Information:
 Part 1 describes the fixed network (stops, routes, lines, etc.)
 Part 2 is mainly focused on timetables
 Part 3 covers fare data
 Part 4 describes the European Profile for NeTEx, focusing on information relevant to feed
passenger information services (excluding operational and fares information)
 Part 5, the NeTEx extension for New Modes, is dedicated to the publication of data concerning
‘Alternative Modes’. It focuses on (but not a limitation to) car sharing, cycle sharing, carpooling,
car/cycle rental. It is primarily oriented towards static data (describing the service that is offered
and associated infrastructure, more than its current running status). The corresponding realtime information is provided by SIRI.
Figure 1: Part 5 extension of NeTEx
This NeTEx extension is based on the Transmodel extension for New Modes (Transmodel is the data
model, and NeTEx the exchange protocol). Additional input (GBFS, DATEX II, OCPI, MDS, etc.) will also
be taken into account. NeTEx already covers a lot of the needs to describes new modes (Site,
Location, Role, Connection, Trip, Journey, Vehicle Type, Parking, Passing Time, etc.). Therefore, New
Modes mostly reuses concepts and just adds a few attributes and concept to the existing ones.

4. INSPIRE – MMTIS
The aim of the INSPIRE-MMTIS study (2019) is to identify and analyse the overlaps and gaps existing
among the relevant standards to be used for the sharing and reuse of data under the remit of
Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/1926 on the provision of EU-wide multimodal travel
information services (MMTIS).
The overall objective of the study, elaborated by experts on each applicable standard (i.e.
Transmodel, NeTEx, DATEX, TAP-TSI, IATA, INSPIRE), is to identify and analyse the overlaps and gaps
existing among the relevant standards. Specifically, the study:
 provides a definition for each data category (in the Annex of the MMTIS Regulation the data
categories are just listed without any definition);
 for each data category, identifies one “reference standard” and one or more “contributing
standard(s)” ; and
 formulates recommendations for both data providers and data users on how to deal with the
overlaps/gaps, including the use of mapping tables when relevant.
Although the MMTIS Regulation mentions INSPIRE as the mandatory source of requirements only for
spatial networks, the analysis reveals that INSPIRE can play the role of reference/contributing
standard also for other data categories than the Road Network, e.g. for Address identifiers or Points
of interest. A methodology is applied to identify and then classify different types of overlaps and to
produce mapping tables from a contributing standard towards a reference standard.
Based on the developed methodology and use cases, recommendations are formulated to both NAP
data consumers and data providers for each data category. For data consumers, recommendations
represent a guidance to select the most suitable datasets among those published in the NAPs; for
NAP data providers, recommendations represent a guidance for publication aimed to avoid semantic
duplication in the data. Finally, some recommendations are also formulated to the EU Member
States and/or the Commission, which identify the need for further developments in order to increase
data interoperability across the different standards.

5. Interoperability
Working Group 2 (WG2) aims to enhance the compatibility of Member States’ NAP data and features
as well as the harmonisation of levels of service. As part of this work WG2 has performed an analysis
of data standards, made an inventory of shortcomings and formulated recommendations concerning
data standards. These are documented in the report below. This also includes data categories in the
field of cycling and cycling infrastructure.
 M2.4_ List of gaps and used standards.pdf (create link to this report)
In the next version of the 2.4 report, the analysis will be extended to include more data standards, as
well as reference profiles and metadata.
