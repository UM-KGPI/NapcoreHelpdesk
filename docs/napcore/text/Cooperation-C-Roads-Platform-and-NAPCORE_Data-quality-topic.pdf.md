1. Introduction
In the dynamic landscape of ITS and C -ITS, mobility-related data plays a central role in enabling effective
and reliable digital services for travelers and road users, following the necessary processing steps. The
NAPCORE initiative and the C -ROADS platform are pivotal in advancing this domain. The NAPCORE
initiative represents a coordinated European effort to harmonize National Access Points (NAPs) across
Europe. Its goal is to enhance the capacity of NAPs to facilitate access to, exchange, and re-use of
standardized mobility-related data collected by European road authorities and transport service operators.
In this context, NAPs serve as central nodes where mobility-related data is concentrated and published in
the form of datasets. Meanwhile, the C -ROADS platform unites European Member States and road
operators to harmonize the deployment of C-ITS services. By ensuring cross-border interoperability, it aims
to improve road safety and efficiency, reinforcing the integration of innovati ve mobility solutions across
Europe.
Recognizing that the delivery of reliable ITS and C -ITS services depends on multiple levels of data,
information, technical, and service quality encompassing the majority of the ITS value chain, from content
collection and processing to service provision and presentation , NAPCORE and C-ROADS have embarked
on a collaborative effort to identify , among others, key intersections between NAPCORE’s data quality
frameworks and C-ROADS services and use cases. This common ground exists in addition to the structural
distinction that ITS services are primarily business-to-business (i.e., between public authorities, operators,
and service providers), whereas C -ITS services address both business -to-business and business -toconsumer contexts. Consequently, while the service dimensions are not directly comparable, the required
data inputs share some common basis.
This collaboration aims to streamline critical dimensions and cooperation potential , including but not
limited to data quality, which are essential for and may impact the delivery of effective end-user services.
To support this broader objective , NAPCORE and C -ROADS formalized their collaboration with a
cooperation agreement signed on May 23, 2023, during the European ITS Congress in Lisbon. This
agreement emphasizes the importance of enhancing the cooperation between NAPs and C -ITS services,
setting forth a framework to ensure that both platforms work together efficiently to support the g rowth
and development of Europe’s mobility data ecosystem.
2. Connection links
The collaboration between NAPCORE and C -ROADS is founded on the recognition that robust and
standardized connection links are essential prerequisites for achieving seamless data exchange and
interoperable services across Europe (on top of other existing requirements related to data processing).
These connection links serve as the pathways through which data flows between NAPs and C-ITS services,
ensuring that information reaches the right end-users reliably, timely, and efficiently. Figure 1 illustrates
the interconnect ed role of NAPCORE and C -ROADS within the ITS value chain : NAPCORE focus es on
harmonizing the content of and access to diverse mobility-related datasets (such as on -street parking,
[page 2]
alternative fuels, MMTIS data, RTTI/SRTI, UVAR, data etc.), while C-ROADS specializes in the provision of
interoperable C-ITS services across European motorways and in urban networks, such as real-time traffic
alerts and road safety notifications.
Description of the various stages of the ITS value chain:
• Content detection and processing: Data is collected from various sources, such as traffic sensors,
weather stations, and probe vehicles. NAPCORE’s primary role in this stage is to support the
detection and standardized provision of this data through the NAPs, ensuring that the information
entering the service ecosystem is accurate, complete, and up -to-date. While some initial
processing may occur (e.g., related to data formatting) , more intensive data processing typically
takes place externally, beyond the boundaries of a NAP , for example at the service provider side.
The service domain can then utilize this data (itself or combined with other data sources) for realtime applications, such as hazard notifications and provision of information, and smart routing .
This stage falls primarily with the scope of NAPCORE. However, the connection between NAPs and
C-ITS services, at this stage, may further promote the accurate, complete, and timely identification
of prevailing traffic/transport conditions and /or on -going events/situations on transport
networks, as a means of providing end-user services with appropriate and valid content.
• Service provisioning: This stage focuses on transforming processed data into operational services
for end-users, such as real -time traffic alerts, route recommendations, or safety messages. The
actual generation of services is typically handled by service providers and lies largely outside the
scope of the collaboration between NAPCORE and C-ROADS. While the quality and consistency of
the underlying data remain s an important enabler, this stage does not explicitly involve joint
coordination efforts.
• Service presentation and delivery to end -users: This final stage concerns the delivery of
information to end-users through various communication channels, such as mobile applications,
in-vehicle systems, and roadside displays. It focuses on ensuring that the information is accessible,
clearly presented, and relevant to the user's context. While this stage lies largely outside the direct
scope of both NAPCORE and C-ROADS, it is supported by the preceding stages of data detection,
processing, and provision. Although C-ROADS provides high-level recommendations on use cases,
its core activities do not include service presentation. Additionally, in the context of C -ITS, endusers can also act as data sources – for instance, vehicles detecting events and generating
messages (e.g., DENMs). This bi -directional flow highlights the collaborative nature of C -ITS but
does not imply that C-ROADS is responsible for the final delivery of information to end-users..

As already outlined above, the ITS value chain implies several quality dimensions that help maintain a high
standard of data management and usability across the entire ITS and C -ITS ecosystem. A preliminary
understanding of these levels is provided below:
Content quality: Refers to the quality of the data itself from a content perspective . It ensures that the
information collected and processed represents appropriately the real-world conditions. Key aspects of
content quality include a) accuracy ( data shall precisely reflect real-world conditions), b) completeness
(data should cover all possible situations and measurable aspects as well as the required locations and
parts of transport networks ), c) timeliness ( data shall be updated frequently to reflect the current
conditions), etc.1
Structural quality: Addresses the consistency, stability, and compliance of the data provisioning process.
This dimension encompasses the technical continuity of the data provisioning service as well as its latency,
compliance with commonly agreed standards, specifications, protocols, and security requirements 2.
Structural quality is essential for enabling seamless communication across different machines, the ultimate
goal of which is to serve the needs of end-users.

3. Quality frameworks
The current section aims to provide an overview of the quality frameworks that are developed in the frame
of NAPCORE. This activity responds to the need for harmonizing the approaches utilized by NAP operators
and ITS data providers to monitor and report the quality of their provided (data
A Quality Framework (QF) is understood as a structured set of definitions and guidelines that outline
quality expectations and assessment methods for ITS data and data services. It includes quality criteria –
that are associated with specific indicators, metrics, or parameters – that help specify minimum
requirements for each ITS data type4. Quality levels define the thresholds that must be met, while
assessment methods provide different approaches for quantifying the above -mentioned indicators,
metrics, and parameters.

the QFs that have been developed in the frame of NAPCORE , including their functional scope and the
legacy of QFs available by EU-EIP .

4. Relation with C-ROADS services and use cases
This section aims to investigate in further detail the connection between NAPCORE and C-ROADS platforms
on the topic of data quality. This is based on the identification of which C -ROADs services/use cases are
relevant with which QF developed in the frame of NAPCORE/EU -EIP . This relation can then fuel further
discussions on lower-level aspects, such as additional quality criteria/requirements or even implications
of data quality aspects on C -ITS service provision (and service quality there of). Table 2 presents the
preliminary results of this analysis.
It is noted that i) Network Topology, ii) Cross-domain, and iii) C-ITS QFs are addressed as horizontal quality
frameworks. This is based on the premise that: a) network topology constitutes the digital foundation for
collecting information, geo -referencing information, and locating information recipients, b) the Cross -
domain QF represents the level of “structural quality” mentioned in Section 2 and the need for
“documentation soundness”, and c) the C-ITS QF provides criteria, metrics, requirements, and assessment
procedures for a wide range of (mainly) Day-1 C-ITS services. For this reason, they appear frequently across
all C-ROADS services and use cases that are related to NAP data and data services.

The purpose of this use case is to provide guidance and

message received by the infrastructure.

The drivers may provide consent to share event data
from the vehicle. The in -vehicle system (i.e., V -ITS-S)
sends out event data that was detected by the vehicle
itself or manually by the drivers. Drivers may also report
event data via their personal devices (e.g., smartphones).
The road operator (or the service provider) collects and
processes the event data sent out by the in -vehicle
systems or reported by the drivers to improve traffic
management and safety operations on the public road
network.

Functional Description of Points of Interest (POI)
Parking Availability
Information (POI-PA)
The use case aims to provide to drivers of all vehicles
(real-time) information related to parking places
(location, availability, services, rates, etc) as well as
potential specific information on parking spaces. This
could be about parking lots on the hi ghway as well in
urban areas. In this use case there is a distinction
between information on the aggregated area/facility
level versus specific parking spot information by
scenario. For further clarity in this use case description
the following definitions are used:
• Parking place (a.k.a. parking lot, parking facility) = a
place or location used for parking, loading, unloading,
standing, or some other mobility or transport related
activity. Place typically identifies a parking structure,
surface lot or on street parking zone.
• Parking space (a.k.a. parking spot) = a single space for
parking, usually designed for one vehicle, which may, but
not necessarily, be denoted by painted or other road
surface marker.

5. Outlook and next steps
Future collaboration between the two platforms on data quality should follow a phased approach to
deepen the understanding of the connection between data quality and consistency of data inputs in terms
of static, semi static and dynamic data while maximizing the resulting benefits.
The initial phase will focus on identifying “high-priority” static and semi static data contents and their next
links to service generation and C-ITS use cases and scenarios.
Building on this foundation, the data content analysis may also be extended to the feedback loops from
users and the positive quality cycle included in this additional process steps and definitions. It could also
reveal whether certain C -ITS services and their specifications necessitate extending existing quality
[page 12]
frameworks and especially feedback cycles , adopting a “bottom-up” approach from users and service
providers tailored to specific use cases.
These investigations are envisioned to take place through targeted workshops involving C -ROADS and
NAPCORE partners, experts, and representatives. Each workshop will concentrate on one or two priority
use cases and their respective data categories and inputs, fostering focused and actionable insights.
