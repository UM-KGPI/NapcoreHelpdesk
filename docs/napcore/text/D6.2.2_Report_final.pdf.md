Abstract
This deliverable represents the Feasibility Study and Operational Roadmap for the European National
BOdy Support System (ENBOSS), developed within Task 6.2 of the NAPCORE project (WG6 –
Compliance Assessment and National Bodies). ENBOSS is proposed as a centralised workflow platform
to support National Bodies (NBs) in conducting compliance assessments of multi-national service and
data providers under the Delegated Regulations of the ITS Directive (2010/40/EU) , addressing the
fragmentation, duplication of effort and inconsistent outcomes that currently characterise cross -
border compliance assessment.
Building on milestone M5.10 (2025) and the results of the WG6 Member State questionnaire, the study
assesses the operational feasibility, resource availability, strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, threats
and estimated costs of implementing ENBOSS. The analysis confirms a clear operational need and
growing interest among NBs, while highlighting that operational maturity and resource capacities vary
considerably across Member States. It concludes that ENBOSS is operationally, technically and
strategically feasible, provided implementation follows a gradual, phased approach based on voluntary
participation and supported by adequate governance, legal and technical frameworks. The study sets
out the key preconditions, cost estimates and recommended next steps for the further development
of ENBOSS within NAPCORE.

1. Introduction
General description of ENBOSS
ENBOSS (European National BOdy Support System) is a centralized platform that manages the end-toend workflow for self-declarations and compliance assessments, providing a standardized mechanism
for data providers, National Bodies, and NAPCORE to communicate and process relevant documents.
It ensures consistency across participating bodies by handling submissions of self-declarations, random
selection for compliance assessments, document management, and final compliance reporting.
The challenge
National Bodies (NBs) across Europe face significant challenges when performing compliance
assessments (CA) for multi-national organisations under the ITS Delegated Regulations. As confirmed
by both Milestone M5.10 (2025) and the analysis of the WG6 Member State questionnaire, the core
difficulty lies in the complexity of compliance assessment procedures involving multi -national
organisations.
These cases require coordination across Member States, alignment of interpretations, and consistent
handling of self-declarations and assessment results — all of which place additional operational and
organisational pressure on NBs and make multi-national CA particularly demanding.
The main challenges can be summarised as follows:
• Limited resources, lack of practical experience, and uneven maturity of National Bodies
Many National Bodies (NBs) across Europe operate with constrained human and technical
resources, which limits their capacity to establish robust national compliance assessment routines
and to handle complex cases involving multi-national organisations. Questionnaire results further
show that a large share of NBs have not yet implemented regular national compliance assessment
processes and remain in a planning or preparatory phase. For these NBs, multi-national compliance
assessment is therefore not an advanced extension of existing practice, but an additional layer of
complexity on top of missing national experience. This situation is compounded by uneven
maturity levels across Member States, where differences in established procedures, expertise, and
operational readiness make coordinated handling of multi -national organisations difficult and
increase the risk of fragmented processes and inconsistent outcomes.
• Fragmented and non-harmonised processes
Where compliance assessments do exist, they are organised differently across Member States,
with variations in interpretation, templates, timing, and documentation requirements. This
fragmentation becomes especially problematic once providers operate across borders.
• Risk that a multi -national organisation fails to adequately acknowledge a non -compliance
finding from a single Member State
By coordinating their efforts NBs can strengthen their position when engaging with multinational
stakeholders, ensuring that compliance assessments are more credible and less burdensome.
Key message
The challenges faced by National Bodies when conducting compliance assessments for multi-national
organisations are structural and operational. The ENBOSS system has been designed precisely to
address these challenges. By centralising the submission of sel f-declarations, enabling coordinated
multi-national compliance assessments, and supporting harmonised procedures and communication
flows, ENBOSS significantly reduces administrative burden and the risk of inconsistent outcomes. More
importantly, it strengt hens the collective position of National Bodies when engaging with multi -
national organisations and enhances the credibility, consistency, and efficiency of compliance
assessment across Europe.

In this sense, ENBOSS is not merely a technical platform, but a practical operational tool designed to
make the conduct of compliance assessment for multi -national organisations substantially more
manageable, coherent, and efficient for National Bodies.
What ENBOSS is NOT: ENBOSS is not an automated tool that directly evaluates data or checks the
technical compliance of data sets; rather, it is strictly a management and workflow platform designed
to coordinate and streamline the assessment process between providers and National Bodies.
Functional Components and Data Flow
ENBOSS consists of five modules that form a complete end -to-end workflow, covering submission,
validation, selection, assessment, and reporting. They enable ENBOSS to function as a centralised,
harmonised system for multinational compliance management across the EU.
Submission of SD at NAP
This part the initial entry point of the process, which primarily takes place at the National Access Point
(NAP) and is therefore not considered as part of the ENBOSS system. Service or data providers submit
their self-declarations (SDs) via the NAP where available, and the system distinguishes whether the
submission is national or multinational. For multinational cases, th is part ensures that SDs are
transferred or redirected into ENBOSS, either automatically (via system integration) or manually (via
user redirection). For automatic integration between ENBOSS and NB/NAP systems an API interface
shall be defined in future containing specification for automatic data exchange. Proposed API interface
shall be in line with NAPCORE proposed NB Reference architecture. The key function of this part of
system is therefore to act as a bridge between national systems and the ENBOSS platform, establishing
the foundation for harmonised EU-level processing.

Module 2 – Random Selection and Preliminary Assessment
The goal of this module is to select a self -declaration in alignment with relevant NBs and national

not yet available) and to perform the theoretical assessment. ENBOSS, in coordination with NAPCORE,

SD is still valid and requesting any missing or outdated accompanying documents. This module ensures
that only relevant, up-to-date, and sufficiently complete cases proceed to the more resource-intensive
in-depth compliance assessment phase.
Module 3 – Assigning and Conducting the Compliance Assessment
Module 3 governs the core compliance assessment process, where one or more National Bodies are
selected and assigned to conduct an in -depth evaluation. ENBOSS coordinates the assignment,
provides access to all relevant documentation, and ensures that all actors fol low a harmonised
assessment methodology (link to Guidelines for a harmonised Compliance Assessment). During this
phase, ENBOSS also facilitates communication between National Bodies and providers, reducing
duplication and ensuring efficient information exchange. The module concludes with the aggregation
and review of assessment results, leading to a single, consolidated compliance outcome agreed at the
European level.
Module 4 – Reporting Results and Follow‐Up
Module 4 focuses on the finalisation and communication of compliance assessment outcomes.
ENBOSS records the consolidated results and notifies relevant stakeholders (NAP, NB, service or data
providers) whether the data set is compliant or non-compliant. In addition, the module supports a
continuous improvement cycle, where non-compliant providers are required to implement corrections
and resubmit updated information. This enables iterative reassessment and ensures that compliance
is maintained over time, reinforcing the system’s role in ongoing monitoring and regulatory
enforcement.

2. Operational Feasibility
This chapter assesses the operational feasibility of establishing and implementing the European
National B Ody Support System (ENBOSS) as a multi -national support framework for compliance
assessment activities under the ITS Delegated Regulations. The assessment is based on the ENBOSS
prototype developed within milestone M5.10, the questionnaire results collected from National
Bodies, and discussions carried out within WG6 and Task 6.2 activities in NAPCORE.
The analysis focuses on operational readiness, expected benefits, implementation challenges and the
conditions necessary for successful deployment of ENBOSS across participating Member States (MSs).
Existing operational basis and readiness of NBs
A significant operational foundation for ENBOSS has already been established through M5.10, which
developed a detailed prototype of harmonised workflows for multi -national self-declarations and
compliance assessments. The proposed concept defines processes for submission and validation of
self-declarations, assignment of responsible NBs, communication with stakeholders, reporting of
results and follow -up activities. It introduces the basis fo r harmonised templates, communication
procedures and semi -automated support functionalities, including interoperability with NAPs and
digital support tools intended to simplify compliance assessment procedures and improve consistency
between Member States.
Task 6.2 within NAPCORE further supports operational implementation through activities related to
governance structures, implementation guidelines, stakeholder coordination and support for the NB
Reference Architecture. In the initial phase of the task, ad ditional analysis of milestone M5.10 was
carried out on a module -by-module basis in order to further assess operational workflows, identify
open issues and propose potential improvements to the ENBOSS system and its future implementation
approach.
As part of WP6 activities, a questionnaire was sent out to the Member States to assess their capacity
and operational maturity. The results of the questionnaire show that the operational maturity of NBs
varies significantly. Out of 16 responses related to multi-national compliance assessment activities, 13
NBs indicated that they have not yet carried out such assessments, while 3 reported practical
operational challenges. The most common challenges include lack of harmonised procedures between
Member States, differing national approaches, limited expertise for cross-border cases and insufficient
operational experience. Several respondents also indicated that compliance assessment activities are
still in an early implementation phase at national level.

Expected operational benefits and implementation challenges
The questionnaire analysis identified several important operational benefits that NBs expect from the
implementation of ENBOSS. The most important motivation for participation is the reduction of
administrative burden for both NBs and service or data providers. Respondents also highlighted the
importance of harmonised compliance assessment outcomes across Member States and easier
engagement with multinational organisations operating across several countries.
M5.10 (2025) further highlights that ENBOSS could significantly reduce duplicated compliance
assessment activities, improve consistency of decisions and strengthen cooperation between National
Bodies. The system would also simplify communication with multinational stakeholders by introducing
a single coordinated framework for self-declarations, accompanying documentation and multinational
compliance assessment procedures.
At the same time , several operational barriers and risks were identified during the feasibility
assessment. Respondents expressed concerns regarding potential loss of sovereignty in decision -
making, legal limitations related to cross -border cooperation, integration challenges with national
systems and differences in digital maturity between Member States. Some National Bodies also
highlighted limited staff capacity, insufficient technical expertise and uncertainty regarding the longterm business case for Member States with numbers of multinational stakeholders.
M5.10 additionally identifies risks related to governance, communication, consistency of assessment
outcomes and long-term sustainability of the system. These findings demonstrate that operational
feasibility is closely linked not only to technical impleme ntation, but also to organisational
coordination, legal harmonisation and long-term governance arrangements.

3. SWOT Analysis
This SWOT analysis provides a structured assessment of the ENBOSS system by examining its internal
strengths and weaknesses, as well as the external opportunities and threats that may influence its
successful implementation and long-term impact. The purpose of this analysis is to support informed
decision￼making by identifying the key factors that can enhance or hinder the system’s effectiveness
within the European regulatory and operational landscape.
Strengths
The ENBOSS system demonstrates strong potential to transform compliance processes at the EU level
by introducing a unified and harmonised framework form for multinational compliance assessment.
Its most significant strength lies in replacing fragmented national procedures with a single, coordinated
compliance process, ensuring consistent outcomes across Member States and reducing regulatory
discrepancies.
Operationally, ENBOSS improves efficiency by allowing one-time submission of self-declarations and
eliminating duplicate assessments, supported by automation and centralised workflows. The platform
also enhances transparency and traceability through structured data management, audit trails, and
standardised reporting.
In addition, ENBOSS strengthens collaboration among National Bodies, enabling knowledge sharing
and coordinated decision-making. Its scalable and modular architecture, aligned with EU digitalisation
goals, positions it as a long-term strategic solution that can evolve beyond its initial scope.

Weaknesses
Despite its advantages, ENBOSS faces some internal limitations that could affect implementation and
effectiveness. A key weakness is the complexity of governance, as the system requires coordinated
decision-making across multiple countries with differing administrative and regulatory practices.
The system also demands upfront investment and organisational effort , including technical
integration with National Access Points (NAPs), process alignment, and staff training. Additionally,
uneven readiness among Member States, in terms of technical capability and resources, may lead to
delays or inconsistent adoption.
Legal and operational complexities – particularly related to GDPR compliance and cross-border data
sharing – further complicate implementation. Finally, the system’s effectiveness is highly dependent
on broad participation, meaning that partial engagement can significantly reduce its overall value.
Opportunities
ENBOSS offers considerable opportunities to become a cornerstone of EU-wide digital compliance
management. Its ability to standardise processes and centralise data provides a strong foundation for
establishing harmonised best practices across Europe.
The system can evolve beyond its initial scope by supporting additional regulatory domains, advanced
analytics, and AI-driven validation tools, thereby increasing its strategic relevance. Centralised data
also enables data -driven policymaking and improved regulatory oversight, strengthening decision -
making at both national and EU levels.
ENBOSS further promotes cross-border cooperation and knowledge transfer, helping less mature
National Bodies benefit from shared expertise. Over time, it can deliver cost efficiencies , improve
compliance rates, and potentially attract EU funding and institutional support aligned with digital
transformation goals.
Threats
The success of ENBOSS is influenced by several external risks. One of the most critical is partial
adoption, where limited participation by Member States could undermine harmonisation and
maintain fragmentation.
Legal and regulatory differences across countries remain a persistent threat, as they may limit the
ability to achieve full consistency in compliance assessments. Additionally, the system relies on
ongoing political, financial, and institutional commitment, making it vulnerable to changes in priorities
or funding availability.
From a technical perspective, cybersecurity risks and data protection concerns are significant due to
the centralised handling of sensitive information. Resistance to change among stakeholders and
challenges in integrating with existing national systems may further delay adoption or reduce
effectiveness.
Conclusion SWOT
ENBOSS represents a forward -looking initiative with the potential to become a cornerstone of
harmonised digital compliance across the European Union. The analysis demonstrates that its core
strengths – particularly in efficiency, transparency, and cross-border coordination – directly address
long-standing challenges related to fragmented national processes and inconsistent regulatory
outcomes.
While the system introduces complexity in governance, implementation, and legal alignment, these
challenges are manageable through structured cooperation with NAPCORE , clear frameworks, and

4. Resource Availability
This chapter assesses the availability of resources required for the implementation and operation of
the European National Body Support System (ENBOSS). The assessment is based on the questionnaire
results collected from National Bodies and EU Member States, the operational and technical concepts
developed within M5.10, and discussions carried out within WG6 and Task 6.2 activities in NAPCORE.
The analysis focuses on the availability of human, technical, organisational and financial resources
necessary for establishing a harmonised multi -national compliance assessment framework and
supporting its long-term operation across participating EU Member States.
Existing capacities and resource needs identified through the questionnaire
The questionnaire results indicate that resource availability represents one of the key challenges for
the future implementation of ENBOSS among EU Member States. Several National Bodies identified
limited operational capacities, insufficient expertise for cross-border compliance assessment activities
and lack of practical experience with multinational cases as important constraints.
The strongest resource needs identified through the questionnaire relate to technical integration and
operational support. All respondents answering this question identified the need for technical
integration support, including APIs and guidance related to the Reference Architecture. In addition, 8
respondents identified the need for financial support and additional resources, while 7 respondents
highlighted the importance of legal templates and cooperation agreements as well as training and
capacity-building activities for staff involved in compliance assessment procedures.

In addition, several respondents stated that they are currently unable to fully assess future resource

5. General support for National Bodies
Besides the operational workflow functionalities foreseen within ENBOSS, Task 6.2 also identified the
need for a dedicated support environment for NBs focused on practical implementation, knowledge
exchange and operational guidance. During the preparation of the system concept and discussions
within WG6, it became clear that many challenges faced by NBs are not purely technical, but
operational, organisational and understanding of regulatory requirements. For this reason, the support
layer was intentionally separated from the ENBOSS workflow platform itself.
To address these needs, the NAPCORE Support Centre was established through the joint work of Tasks
T6.2 – Operational Support to National Bodies and T8.2 – NAP Support Center in this phase of
NAPCORE. The Support Centre is available to all project partners through the common NAPCORE
project environment and serves as a central point for practical guidance, knowledge sharing and access
to relevant project resources.
Within the NAPCORE Support Centre, a dedicated National Bodies section already provides the
material needed by National Bodies to carry out compliance assessments of data and service providers
within their respective EU Member States. This includes practic al guidance, harmonised self -
declaration and compliance assessment forms, supporting documents, templates, the National Body
Reference Architecture and other relevant NAPCORE outputs. By bringing these resources together in
one common location, the Support Centre facilitates day-to-day compliance assessment activities and
supports more consistent application of NAPCORE recommendations across Member States.
The structure was intentionally designed to combine static guidance content with collaborative and
interactive elements. In practice, NBs often require quick clarification of operational questions,
interpretation support, examples from other Member States or practical recommendations based on
real implementation experience. For that reason, the platform foresees not only document
repositories, but also sections focused on discussions, experience exchange, “Tips and Tricks”,
stakeholder engagement, common implementation mistakes and practical operational
recommendations.
A major advantage of this approach is its flexibility and long-term maintainability. The environment
can be continuously updated as Delegated Regulations evolve, new operational practices emerge and
additional NAPCORE outputs become available. This is especially important considering the significant
differences in maturity, organisational structures and operational capacities across EU Member States.
The NAPCORE Support Centre therefore complements ENBOSS: while ENBOSS is designed to
coordinate multi-national self-declaration and compliance assessment workflows, the Support Centre
provides the knowledge, guidance and practical support required by Natio nal Bodies for national
compliance assessment activities and for the gradual harmonisation of practices across Europe.

6. Cost Analysis
The ENBOSS system will cover the full process cycle, as described in the Annex of this document. The
system is expected to serve approximately 30 National Bodies and 200 multinational stakeholders,
representing a moderate-scale European digital service.
Estimated Development Costs
The estimated one -time investment for the design, development, and deployment of the ENBOSS
System is between EUR 80,000 and 150,000.
This estimate includes the implementation of a fully functional ENBOSS platform that aligns with
established NAPCORE workflows. It comprises the costs related to the following items:
• system design and architecture
• development of user interfaces tailored to providers, National Bodies, and administrators,
• implementation of the backend workflow engine supporting the lifecycle of self-declarations,
validation processes with feedback loops, coordination of compliance assessments across
multiple National Bodies, and reporting and follow-up functionalities.
The estimated investment also covers the implementation of secure data and document management
with versioning and traceability, automation features such as completeness checks, status tracking and
notifications, as well as role -based access control mechanisms ensuring GDPR -compliant data
handling. In addition, basic interoperability capabilities are included to allow future integration with
National Access Points, together with testing, deployment, and initial system setup.
The relatively moderate cost level is justified by several factors: the system builds on harmonised
NAPCORE processes and templates, which reduces design complexity; it focuses on essential
functionalities only, in line with an MVP approach that limits integration requirements at this stage; it
relies on standard web technologies and a cost -efficient, centralised architecture suited to the
moderate scale of the expected user base; and a phased development approach allows more advanced
functionalities to be added later without increasing the initial investment.
Operational Costs (Annual)
The annual hosting and infrastructure cost is estimated between EUR 5,000 and 15,000. This includes
cloud-based hosting of the application and database, secure storage of documents, system availability,
backup and recovery mechanisms, and baseline cybersecurity measures such as encryption and
controlled access.
Annual maintenance and support costs are estimated between EUR 10,000 and 30,000. These cover
technical maintenance and system updates, ongoing security management and alignment with EU data
protection requirements, functional updates in response to regulatory or operational changes, user
support for National Bodies and providers, and overall system administration.
Total Cost of Ownership
The total cost of ownership consists of an initial development cost of EUR 80,000 to 150,000 and annual
operational costs ranging between EUR 15,000 and 45,000. Based on these estimates, the indicative
total cost over a three-year period is between EUR 125,000 and 285,000.

7. Feasibility conclusions
This report confirms that ENBOSS represents a realistic and strategically relevant approach for
supporting multi-national compliance assessment activities across Europe. The analysis carried out
through M5.10, the WG6 questionnaire and Task 6.2 activities confirm both a clear operational need
and growing interest among National Bodies, while important technical and organisational foundations
– including a modular workflow, harmonised templates, digital self -declaration concepts and NB
Reference Architecture activities – have already been established within NAPCORE.
Successful implementation will depend on several preconditions, particularly governance
arrangements, legal coordination, interoperability with national systems and data protection
requirements. Given the varying levels of operational readiness across Member States, the preferred
cooperation model – voluntary participation supported by clear coordination mechanisms – indicates
that ENBOSS should initially be positioned as a cooperative support framework rather than a
mandatory centralised system.
As confirmed by the SWOT analysis, the long-term opportunities associated with ENBOSS significantly
outweigh the identified risks, and the remaining governance, maturity and legal challenges are
considered manageable through phased implementation and conti nuous stakeholder engagement.
Overall, ENBOSS is operationally, technically and strategically feasible under current conditions,
representing a realistic next step towards a more harmonised and sustainable European approach to
multi-national compliance assessment under the ITS Delegated Regulations.
8. Recommendations and next steps
The feasibility assessment confirms that ENBOSS should continue to be developed gradually through
a phased and flexible implementation approach, building on the clear operational need and positive
interest expressed by National Bodies for stronger European coordination.
Future activities should prioritise establishing clear governance structures, legal frameworks and
interoperability mechanisms – including GDPR compliance, cross-border cooperation agreements and
alignment with the NB Reference Architecture – alongside continued technical integration support,
semi-automated tools and capacity-building for participating National Bodies. Within WG6 and Task
6.2, this includes further development of digital self -declaration functionalities, APIs, harmonised
workflows, training activities and exchange of operational experience between Member States.
Given the varying maturity levels and resource capacities across Member States, ENBOSS should
initially operate as a voluntary and cooperative support framework, allowing gradual onboarding
according to national priorities. The next phase of development sh ould therefore focus on
transforming the existing conceptual and procedural foundation established through M5.10 into a
practical operational support framework that strengthens cooperation, reduces administrative burden
and improves harmonisation across Europe.

Annex
Justification
The introduction of ENBOSS would significantly reduce the compliance burden currently observed
under the fragmented implementation of multiple ITS Delegated Regulations. Today, with an
estimated 400–800 stakeholders —noting that this figure is indicative as no official consolidated
statistics exist at EU level—generating roughly 1,500–3,000 self-declarations, each data provider must
submit separate declarations across multiple National Access Points (NAPs), often duplicating effort
due to differing national implementations, formats, and metadata requirements. Focusing specifically
on multinational service providers, these actors by definition operate in a minimum of 2 countries,
with coverage extending up to approximately 44 countries for pan-European digital platforms, and
an estimated average operational footprint of around 8 –12 countries within this subset. ENBOSS
would streamline this landscape by acting as a centralised brokerage and harmonisation layer ,
enabling a “declare once, reuse everywhere” approach. In practice, this would allow a single,
standardized self-declaration to be validated once and reused across multiple countries , reducing
duplication by an estimated 40–70%, particularly for multinational and digital actors, while also
improving consistency through common templates and automated validation mechanisms.
For example, If Flixbus is operating in 35 Countries in Europe it has to provide SD document for two
data types according to MMTIS:
• 1 for Static Data (NeTEx standard)
• 1 for Dynamic Data (SIRI standard)
Therefore, it should provide 35x2 SD documents = 70 SD document compared to 2 if using ENBOSS
system.
