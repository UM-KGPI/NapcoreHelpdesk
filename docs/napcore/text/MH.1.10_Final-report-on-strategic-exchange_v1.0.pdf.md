1. Introduction
The objective of this report is to give an overview on the work regarding strategic exchange,
cooperation and collaboration with externals and relevant stakeholders supplementing the mid-term
report MH1.6. In NAPCORE the strategic exchange and cooperation with externals is mainly carried out
by two mechanisms:
 The Advisory Board (see chapter 2. Advisory Board)
 Cooperation Agreements (see chapter 3. Cooperation Agreements)
The updates on these two mechanisms are described in the following chapters as well as the plans for
the second funding phase of NAPCORE.
2. Advisory Board
The Advisory Board is an advising body for solicited and unsolicited advice to the NAPCORE Steering
Committee and NAPCORE Activities regarding the project and its interactions in the European ITS
ecosystem. The main task of the Advisory Board is to give overall non-binding strategic advice to serve
the common objectives and vision of NAPCORE. The size and members of the Advisory Board were
defined in cooperation with the EU Commission services. Initial planning took place in September 2021.
2.1. Set up of the Advisory Board
To conceptualise the Advisory Board a specific task force was set up, consisting of all active members
of H.1 and interested NAPCORE partners. The relevant sectors and geographical coverage that should
be covered by the Advisory Board were identified as well as associations and stakeholder groups
relevant for each sector with emphasis on sectors that are not primarily covered by NAPCORE. The
considered associations were narrowed down, specific members were identified, and a time schedule
for the invitation phase was elaborated. In parallel, a document describing the Criteria and Process for
the selection of NAPCORE Advisory Board Members was developed (see 5.Annex I).
After suitable associations were identified in the concept phase, H.1 partners reached out to the
potential members to identify their availability and interest in joining the Advisory Board. Afterwards,
bilateral meetings were held with every association.
The current Advisory Board members in alphabetical order are:

2.2. Advisory Board meetings in 2022
The Advisory Board constitutional meeting took place on 7 June 2022 virtually and included a short
introduction to NAPCORE and an explanation of the Advisory Board set up. “Rules of play” were
established and the possibilities for interaction, as well as the added value for Advisory Board Members
were presented. A slot for interactive work was included as well as a discussion about strategic future
topics. The second Advisory Board Meeting took place at the NAPCORE Mobility Data Days on 2
November 2022 in Paris. A brief update on the status of the NAPCORE activities was given, followed
by an interactive World Café workshop on the following four topics:
 Topic 1: Role of the NAPs in a Mobility Data Space
 Topic 2: Future of NAPCORE
 Topic 3: Implications of the revision of the RTTI DR - implications for private parties
 Topic 4: Implementation of the MMTIS and revised RTTI DR – extension of the geographical
Scope
For more information on Chapters 2.1 and 2.2 please have a look at report MH.1.6. The slides and
inputs of all meetings are documented in a Conceptboard (restricted access only for Advisory Board
members and NAPCORE partners).
2.3. Advisory Board Meetings in 2023
The third Advisory Board meeting took place virtually on 24 April 2023. It started with a general project
update and an overview of NAPCORE’s achievements. The ambassador work, as well as the status of
Cooperation Agreements were presented. The following topics were discussed during the interactive
work:
 MMTIS revision statement paper
These were the comments from Advisory Board members: It was noted that the duration, for
which a dataset has to remain on the NAP, has not yet been discussed. This may be examined within
NAPCORE after the publication of the revised delegated regulation, since this will not be detailed in
the delegated regulation itself. APIs (Application Programming Interfaces) for booking and payment
will most probably be part of the MDMS (Multimodal digital mobility services) and will not be covered
within the Delegated Regulation. The European Commission will discuss these topics with different
experts. The specific time a dataset has to stay on the NAP will be decided in reference to other
documents like the passenger rights regulation. The revised delegated regulation will not include any
mandatory collection of new data.

These were the comments from Advisory Board members: EPA is working on a detailed
overview of the parking data already available on the NAPs as well as an overview of the parking data

notes that the mobility management for users is their focus, especially tools to influence mobility in

The fourth Advisory Board meeting was organized in conjunction with the NAPCORE Mobility Data
Days on 7 November 2023 in Budapest, Hungary. The General Secretary gave an overview of the
NAPCORE Mobility Data Days as well as the status of NAPCOREs work and achievements. The
remaining time was used to work interactively on the following topics:
 The revised ITS directive and its implications
 Future of NAPCORE
 Standardisation aspects in context of a selected topic
 Use cases for the use of NAP data in support of multi-modal traffic operations
The outcomes are summarised as follows:
The revised ITS directive mentions Mobility-as-a-service, which is a new and broader approach than
earlier. Therefore, the input from the Advisory Board was that NAPCORE should focus more on public
transport data e.g. rail. NAPCORE should be the frontrunner in the harmonisation of NAPs and its data
content. This should be done by making guidelines, especially in relation to technical support, e.g. in
relation to standards or interoperability and describe minimum requirements for instance on NAP
architecture. The goal is to make good quality data easily accessible so that service providers can use
the data. NAPCORE should therefore encourage Member States to implement the mobilityDCAT-AP on
every NAP. NAPCORE should be a platform for the exchange of experience and knowledge, as well as
for showcasing best cases. Additionally, marketing NAPCORE more intensely should be a goal.
NAPCORE shall be sure that no other projects are doing the same, e.g. in relation to EMDS.
One important tip regarding the future of NAPCORE was that a clear plan for the future is necessary
before the structure can be chosen. Identifying the unique selling point was suggested. It was very
clearly indicated that an AISBL (Association international sans but lucratif) is not a good option. Most
green dots were added to the European agency. It was highlighted that a linkage to the EC is important
because that ensures that the content is Member State driven. Also, the enlargement of the
geographical scope will be important and has to be figured out. Timo Hoffmann mentions that research
will be done regarding an informal group for Member States. The input will be integrated in the further
work regarding the long-term governance structure of NAPCORE in 2024.
It was recommended to investigate how to improve the standardization process by CEN, because it
may pose a potential risk to the ITS Directive and the associated Delegated Regulations. The
participants discussed that the process of CEN could jeopardise the Delegated Regulations. This is not
urgent but has to be taken into account. In the rail sector conflicting regulations are in force. This needs
to be addressed. The ITS domain has over 200 standards and more are coming. It was suggested that
these standards should be maintained as part of a cohesive ecosystem. Particular emphasis was placed
on the importance of standards for vulnerable road users. Also maps need to be better supported by
the standards. Overall, the process was positively received.
The objective of the fourth topic was to present some added value scenarios how data from NAPs can
be utilized (see also M3.12 European NAPs added value scenarios in key application areas). One
important learning from these use cases was that it is not trivial to access data across the different
NAPs. In some cases, a lot of time was required to access the datasets. It was also highlighted that
parking data is not well represented on the NAPs (see study from EPA) and that other modes of
transport besides road shall be included more often. Some scenarios were suggested and critical
success factors for NAPs were mentioned.

3. Cooperation Agreements
Cooperation Agreements are the basis for individual involvement of external organisations in
NAPCORE. The goal is to foster interaction and discussions with those organisations on operational
(Working Group) level. In addition to the Advisory Board, which is on overall strategic level, this will
add valuable input to technical aspects of NAPCORE. To have a written basis for Cooperation
Agreements within NAPCORE, a short document called “Background, process and criteria for the
establishment of Cooperation Agreements between NAPCORE and externals” was elaborated.
In total, NAPCORE signed six Cooperation Agreements with the following organisations and
associations:
 CIE - Cycling Industries Europe
 EPA - European Parking Association
 C-Roads - The Platform of harmonised C-ITS deployment in Europe
 DFRS - Data for Road Safety
 POLIS - Cities and Regions for Transport innovation
 And one Agreement on Safety Related Traffic Information (SRTI) with following partners:
o TISA - Traveller Information Services Association
o DATEX II community as part of NAPCORE
o DFRS - Data for Road Safety ecosystem
o C2C-CC - Car-2-Car Communication Consortium
o C-Roads - The Platform of harmonised C-ITS deployment in Europe
More information on the first three listed Cooperation Agreements can be found in the mid-term
report MH.1.6.
3.1. New Cooperation Agreements
Since the last report MH.1.6, three of the above-mentioned Cooperation Agreements were finalised
and signed. Further details on these new Cooperation Agreements are listed below.
3.1.1. Data for road safety
The Cooperation Agreement with DFRS should foster the exchange of intelligence on availability of
data of relevance for road safety. DFRS and NAPCORE agree to cooperate on the following activities:
 Exchange of best practices in terms of availability of data that are relevant for road safety
purposes
 Exchange and upgrade of knowledge on the availability and use of SRTI data at the NAPs
 Exchange on lessons learnt from the governance set-up and working of each other`s ecosystem
(contractual framework, regulatory policy, other legal issues)
 Benchmarking of the performance of data sharing mechanisms, lessons learnt, enablers,
blocking factors (aggregated/anonymized, with due respect to confidentiality)
 Dissemination of each other`s ecosystem and its working
 Development of joint activities and actions with the aim of improving the quality and
accessibility of road safety related data
 Jointly work on SRTI-related content and data modelling and profiling in relevant standards
 Jointly work on the ways pan-European SRTI datasets are made findable and accessible on the
NAPs in an efficient and reliable way

3.1.2. POLIS
In general, the Cooperation Agreement with POLIS aims to enhance the dialogue with data and service

The following goals are listed in the Cooperation Agreement:
NAPCORE and POLIS agree to

 Collect requirements and needs of cities and regions in order to define suitable support
measures for efficient data provision (by means of 1 or 2 questionnaires or 1 or 2 joint
workshops)
 Jointly create and disseminate information and training material for data and service providers

 Organise joint trainings on data exchange standards focussed on cities and regions
3.1.3. Safety Related Traffic Information (SRTI)
TISA, NAPCORE, DATEX II, DFRS, C2C-CC and C-ROADS have entered into this Agreement to jointly work
on the harmonization of Safety Related Message Sets for Safety Related Traffic Information (SRTI). This
Agreement concerns the document “Safety related message sets – Selection of DATEX II Situations,
DENM and TPEG2-TEC Causes and TMC Events for EC high level Categories” and any related work. The
aim is to contribute to a fully interoperable technical ecosystem supporting the policy aims of the
European SRTI Delegated Regulation.
The Parties agree to:
 Accept joint ownership of the work results produced under this Agreement
 Continue the work on the harmonization of SRTI message sets and any supplementary
documentation
 Participate actively in joint activities, such as working group or task forces, to improve and
extend the work

3.2.6. Safety Related Traffic Information (SRTI)
The Cooperation Agreement between NAPCORE, DATEX II, TISA, C-ROADS, DFRS and C2C-CC was
approved by the NAPCORE Steering Committee in March 2024 and formally signed at the Connecting
Europe Days in April 2024.
A joint working group was established to work on the revision of the core product of this collaboration:
Safety related message sets – Selection of DATEX II Situations, DENM rel. 2 and TPEG2-TEC Causes and
TMC Events for EC high level Categories of Road Safety related Traffic Information – SRTI.
The reason for this revision is the rapidly increasing ability and availability of vehicles to generate safety
related information as an important data source to improve the quality of safety related information
services and therefore the safety on European roads. Where up until this version of the document the
flow of data was mainly oriented from road operator towards service providers, now a new
cooperative data source becomes available in the connected vehicles and mobility domain, which use
for good reason the C-ITS or other standards to generate consistent information services for travellers.
With this development in the connected vehicle context other relationships between the standard
data formats used become more explicitly relevant: This is the transformation from complete
messages between the data format standards involved in an unambiguous way and without the loss
of information will be more relevant and being processed automatically.
Version 4 of the aforementioned document, published by the group in 2025, now contains a section
about how the DFRS initiative supports the growing ecosystem of Safety Related Traffic Information
(SRTI) together with references related to location referencing and clarifications for some of the event
codes.

5. Annex I
Criteria and Process for the selection of
NAPCORE Advisory Board Members
The Advisory Board is an advising body for solicited and unsolicited advice to the NAPCORE Steering
Committee and NAPCORE Activities regarding the project and its interactions in the European ITS
ecosystem. The main task of the Advisory Board is to give overall non-binding strategic advice to serve
the common objectives and vision of NAPCORE.
An involvement on a regular basis is expected already at early stages of the strategic NAPCORE
processes, with input mainly concerning:
 Concept for a long-term governance structure
 Options for data access and usage on the NAPs
 Identification of new specific sectoral issues of strategic importance for NAPCORE
 Steps towards the integration of NAPs into the European mobility data space
This also entails the promotion of NAPCORE and the dissemination of the elaborated findings within the
own sectoral networks. The Advisory Board will be installed as a defined group of up to 30 persons.
Criteria for Members of the Advisory Board
The following criteria will be applied for the selection of Advisory Board Members
 Representing a multi-national stakeholder group with a European interest or being a renowned
expert having a known status in a relevant network; representing one or multiple key sectors:
 Safety
 Digitalisation technologies (C-ITS, UVAR, Map Providers, …)
 Data providers (transport service provider, infrastructure operators, charging
infrastructure, …)
 Cities
 Multimodal transport, sharing/pooling services
 Freight and logistics
 Service provider
 IT, data cloud, data space
 Standards
 Economy and competitiveness
 National Body/National Authority - Legal issues, privacy, security
 Environment
 Ability to communicate sectoral positions to NAPCORE and vice-versa
 Availability and participation in regular meetings (about twice a year)
In order to ensure a relevant sectoral representativeness, the Advisory Board will consist of different
(non-profit) associations, representing relevant stakeholder groups like global private players, data and
service providers, industry and (end-)users as well as ambassadors of flagship (data) projects which
should cover relevant key domains. By involving only associations, the geographical coverage across
Europe is well addressed and the risk of specific company influences is minimized.
The respective key domains were defined in alignment with the ITS Directive and the Delegated
Regulations, in close cooperation with the EU Commission services as well as the NAPCORE Steering
Committee. A consistent representation across the sectors is the aim for the Advisory Board.

Process for selection of Advisory Board Members
The list of Advisory Board Members will be managed by the Deputy General Secretary of NAPCORE.
The composition of the Advisory Board can be adapted according to the respective needs of NAPCORE.
In case some members are not contributing on a regular basis, they might be excluded, whilst new
members can be included in the Advisory Board. The following two processes can be applied for
constituting the Advisory Board or becoming a new member:
1. Active invitation to the Advisory Board:
Based on the identified key domains, relevant associations are determined and screened for
potential membership in terms of a solid sectoral representation. Identified potential members will
be addressed (informing them about the underlying selection process as well as related
duties/expectations) and actively invited by the Deputy General Secretary, the Steering Committee
or a Core Alignment Team Member of NAPCORE.
2. Application / Nomination for the Advisory Board:
It is possible to actively apply as a member or to nominate suitable candidates for the Advisory
Board. The potential members will then be assessed regarding their representation of the identified
key domains and suitability for the Advisory Board. In case of a favourable decision by the Core
Alignment Team, the Deputy General Secretary, the Steering Committee or a Core Alignment Team
Member of NAPCORE will actively address and invite the applicant/nominee.
