1. Mobility Data Days – Paris – cycling session - 2nd November 2022
“CYCLING AS A KEY USE CASE FOR NAPS”
driven by Cristina BURAGA (Cerema) and Ronald Jorna (Moveco), Ambassadors for cycling and cycling
infrastructure data in NAPCORE
12 participants, special session reserved for NAPCORE members
Objective:
Since NAPs have been made numerous datasets accessible, the question raises how to increase their
use and reuse. Given the increased interest in cycling as a green transport mode, at EU, national and
local level, also the interest in cycling data is increasing. It is clear that at present cycling is not as much
present in the mobility data as is the case in motorized transport. Therefore, cycling and cycling
infrastructure data looks a promising use case.
This special session identifies the position of cycling data in NAPCORE and explore to what extent NAPs
are already today being used for cycling applications and how to increase their usefulness for cycling.
Various speakers active in the collection and use of cycling data were invited, in order to share their
experience and explain their aspirations to build a common working structure at European level.
Program:
After the presentation of the Ambassadors for cycling and cycling infrastructure data and their role
within the Napcore project, several speakers explored the issues of cycling data in the NAPs:
1. “Strategic perspective for cycling data development and the need to collaborate” - Kevin
Mayne, Chief Executive of “Cycling Industries Europe” (CIE), an international association
for cycling businesses, that brings together Expert Groups on key industry topics, including
ITS and cycling intelligence
2. “A short introduction of the French strategy and feedback” - Yohan Planche representative
of the “Cycling and walking mission”, at the Ministry in charge of Transports
3. “How to develop cycling thanks data ” - Stéphanie Mangin, Head Officer of the
"observation" unit at Vélo & Territories org., responsible for cycling data collection and
analysis (from infrastructure to traffic) in charge of the National Platform of bicycle Traffic
4. “How to ease private service providers sharing their data on the NAPs? ” - Vincent
Dieulesaint, Engineering Manager at Eco-Counter, in charge of the team developing
software analysis solutions and services to supply traffic data

Conclusion:
This special “cycling session” organized at the first Mobility Data Days (2 nd November 2022 in Paris),
allowed to give an overview of the stakeholders involved in the cycling domain, both public authorities
and private partners, and to understand their aspirations towards the project NAPCORE in its role as a
coordination interface and facilitation of cycling data exchanges.
 The French Ministry of Transport created a specific mission for “Cycling and walking”, in order
to encourage soft modes and increase their modal share in traffic. Various subsidy programs
allow French regions and cities to develop cycling infrastructure and set up experiments for
new alternative mobility. In particular, several public and private partners driven by the
Ministry have developed a national standard for cycling counting, infrastructure and parking.
 Among the partners involved in the cycling standard implementation, the association “Velo &
Territoires” brings together more than 200 members with the aim to develop the use of
bicycles in all territories and set up a “National Frequentation Platform” (PNF) which aggregate
bicycle information at the national level on the basis of counting data collected at the local
level. Their challenges concern technical issues on data availability, standards, indicators…, and
strategical due to variety of actors for data provision, also acceptability, interoperability issues.
 Cycling Industry Europe (CIE) represent the world’s leading cycling companies with the
objective of building a European cycling consortium, to promote cycling within other
sustainable mobility strategies, particularly in the context of multimodal transport MMTIS
more broadly, in complementarity to other soft modes of transport such as walking.
 An example of a private provider of technical solutions, market-leader in the cycling
measurement, Eco-counter presented the difficulties and solutions for counting soft modes
such as cycling, compared to motorized traffic in terms of technological devices (variety of
cycle types) or user behavior (non-linear trajectories, ride abreast, platoons…)
All of these presentations showed overall the need to create an exchange interface about cycling data,
which can be played by the NAPCORE project via the cycling Ambassadors, in order to allow better
consideration in the NAPs and increase their visibility within mobility policies at European level. More
concretely, this first “cycling session” gave directions of work for the cycling Ambassadors concerning
internal/external communication with technical experts and strategical partners of NAPCORE.

2. Webinar - 18 April 2023
“KEY CYCLING DATA STANDARDISATION PRIORITIES”
Moderators: Jørgen Wanscher (Hermes Traffic Intelligence), Bard de Vries (NDW Netherlands)
Organisers: Ambs Cristina BURAGA (Cerema) and Ronald Jorna (Moveco), with Sam Pierce (CIE)
Number of attendees: 21 participants (NAPCORE, industry, authorities)
Objective:
The main objective of this workshop reserved for the cycling working-group involved in the project
NAPCORE, is to prioritize the type of data that needs to be standardized, with a focus on counting,
infrastructure and bike parking data (bike sharing is covered by SWG4.3 MMTIS).
Program:
In the 1 st part, 6 speakers were invited to present standards and protocols for mapping and routing,
definitions of cycling infrastructure, bike parking and counting:
1. Mapping, routing and turn by turn navigation (Antoine Laporte Weywada, Géo Vélo). In
France a national standard for bikeway description has been developed with a free app
and SaaS platform. It provides comparable metrics across France and is interoperable with
OpenStreetMap. Lateralization of the bike infrastructure is key to describe the
infrastructure. It could be the basis for an EU standard describing bike infrastructure.
2. Counting and Measuring Cycling (Vincent Dieulesaint, EcoCounter). There are mainly three
standards for counting cycling: DATEX II (EU standard), Mobility Counting Schema (France)
and the Federal High Way Standard (a US standard that hasn’t been globally adopted and
only a few US sates use it). These standards are all quite different. For companies and
public authorities, it increases costs of publishing and sharing data in the various formats.
3. Finding Bike Parking and Usage Conditions (Nigel Williams, European Parking Association).
There is already a European standard for parking based on DATEX II. EPA now wants to
include (secure) bike parking in this standard. It does not necessarily have to be one
standard, as a converter between two (or more) standards could be possible. This is
currently the case for parking with the APDS standard and other standards in parking.
4. Informing Cyclists about Real Time (and Future) Disruptions of the Available Cycling
Infrastructure (Reiner Dölger, Rheinland Pfalz). In Germany a new data standard for cycling
infrastructure has been agreed, and Germany is working on a database for RTTI on roads
(data feeds for restrictions, works, event on cycling routes).
5. Delivering Real-Time Traffic Data to Cyclists (Stephanie Chaufton, TISA). TISA would like to
expand their traffic information services with information for cyclist on e.g. traffic events,
traffic flow, weather, bike parking, emergency warnings, e-bike charging, etc. They see a
challenge in location referencing.
6. Integrating Better Cycling Data into Google Maps (Britta Duerscheid, Google). Google has
added cycling infrastructure to their maps, allowing for trip planning indications of hills,
car traffic, road type, available bike lanes, etc. They distinguish between separate bike
lanes, painted bike lanes and shared roads, including information e.g. on road surface,
opening hours, direction. Also bike sharing is included. Important is to have access to high
quality bike lane data. Can NAPCORE be the repository to bike lane information in Europe?
The speakers answered questions on the use-cases for cycling data standardisation (static or dynamic):
i. What is the state of play in your field regarding the availability of the relevant data?
ii. What are the main challenges?
iii. What exactly are the priorities for cycling data standardisation?

Conclusion:
Standardisation of cycling data is especially necessary to improve the efficiency for the ITS industry. If
proper standards are agreed, the ITS industry doesn’t have to make separate interfaces for each new
client, but can use one single standard for all clients. This is not only beneficial for the ITS industry, but
will also lower the costs of their services to local authorities and end users, since it will require less
customized services. Standardisation of cycling data is also important since it will support the
European policy to promote active mobility.
The following steps forward have been formulated:
1. To set up a task force to lead the standardisation of a number of cycling data types. This task
force will be lead by the cycling ambassadors and people mentioned above, possibly extended
with experts in the standardisation cycling domain. The task force will lead the standardisation
process and supervise the working groups.
2. To set up a number of working groups for a specific cycling data type:
 Cycling counting data
 Bicycle parking data
 Bicycle infrastructure data
 Realtime data for cyclist (road works, disruptions, etc.)
The working groups should consist of public authorities (representatives of national platforms
like Vélo-Territoires, Tour de Force, etc.), cycling advocacy groups (e.g. ECF), industry and
standardisation experts. The work in the working groups should be based on requirements and
use case collection from the cycling sector.

3. TN-ITS meeting – ERTICO Brussels hybrid - 26 September 2023
“NEEDS FOR MAP INNOVATION” = > New map content (cycling)
Moderators: Frank Daems (ERTICO)
Ambs cycling: Cristina BURAGA (Cerema)
Cristina Buraga presented the Ambassadors role for the cycling data in NAPCORE to TN-ITS and ERTICO
members, in order to help them to understand what is going on in the cycling domain and what impact
it might have on TN-ITS standard. More specifically, she presented the new content for cycling data
and the needs related to the cycling infrastructure.
In the same way as for classic motorized traffic, we need to map the cycling data, enriched with:
• Types: segregated cycle routes, on-road/on-path shared with vehicles/pedestrians
• Attributes: route surface, gradients, accessibility (forest path, private, etc.), parking
• Users: classic bike, e-bike, mountain bike, trailer, cargo, taxi, etc. and other soft modes
This need concerns:
1. the classification of the cycling infrastructure, according to the speed and traffic volume
(motorized and cyclists vs. cars) for:
• dedicated sites (as cycle routes/paths/tracks, greenways…) allowed only for the soft
modes (cycles, pedestrian, but also personal travel engines, mopeds…)
• shared sites (as cycle lanes/streets, velorues…) allowed for mixed modes:
soft/motorized traffic (cycles, cars, motorcycles…and pedestrians)
2. the classification of users:
• how to define a cycle (in relation to its silhouette, dimensions, speed, other
characteristics)?
• who is a pedestrian (coordination between: road safety, accessibility, metrology,
traffic management, infrastructure…)?
For counting cycling a lot of measurements, indicators and data process could be important to be mapmatched and several use cases may be concerned by standardization.
The work that continued after the webinar made it possible to initiate the merger of Datex II and TN-
ITS standards under the WG4 of the project NAPCORE, with a reflection on possible extensions of these
standards to consider cycling data for the infrastructure mapping.

4. Mobility Data Days – Budapest – cycling session - 8 November 2023
“CHALLENGES AND OPPORTUNITIES FOR CYCLING DATA STANDARDISATION”
Moderators: Jørgen Wanscher (Hermes Traffic Intelligence), Bard de Vries (NDW Netherlands)
Organisers: Ambs Cristina BURAGA (Cerema) and Mirelle PETERS (NTM), with Sam Pierce (CIE)
Number of attendees: 21 participants (NAPCORE, industry, authorities)
Objective:
The Cycling Ambassadors noted the need to harmonize current national standards for cycling and
cycling infrastructure data, and to come to European standards. In the framework of Mobility Data
Days in Budapest, the cycling session explored the challenges and priorities concerning cycling data
standardization (static/dynamic data), by considering stakeholders’ feedback and necessary actions
focused on specific cycling topics (counting, infrastructure, parking, and real-time data).
Program:
After the introduction by the cycling Ambassadors and the presentation by the local authority
representatives from Budapest BKK, in the 1 st part concerning the Challenges, 4 speakers exposed
priorities on the cycling data standardisation (static/dynamic data) for: counting (NDW), infrastructure
(ECF), parking (EPA) and real-time information (HIT).
The 2nd part dedicated to Feedback and opportunities, several policy and associations representatives
(POLIS, TISA, ITxPT, ECF…) talked about their expectations on cycling data needs and perspectives for
future common actions.
The last part allowed exchanges with the assistance on the community building, especially on cycling
data standardization (for who, why and what needs to be standardized).
Conclusion:
The goal of the session was to collect challenges, priorities and value in the standardisation of cycling
data (needs, beneficiaries, types) and to understand how does cycling data relate to other modes of
transport. Among the lessons learned, the main point is the paradigm shift regarding the approach to
data provision, which features different actors from the domain of traffic management: mobility
services, authorities and community representatives, service operators, industry suppliers and
maintainers, associative and user community directly.
If traditionally the cycling data are a municipal competence, there are no homogenous cycling data
between countries and no official standard on cycle infrastructure on the European level (cycle
track/lane/street…). We can even point out that researchers and policymakers desperate for data!
Concerning the availability of the cycling data, there is better consideration in the Delegated
Regulation (EU) 2017/1926 on the MMTIS, which allows better interfacing with other transport
intramodality services (public transport, parking, sharing…). But for the cycling infrastructure, the big
question remains the street/road safety for cycles in mixed traffic (without segregated infrastructure).
This implies an expansion of the community, in order to take into account the different stakeholders
involved in traffic management, road safety, logistic or real-time travelers information.
In this perspective, the cycling Ambassadors and the Taskforce organize a series of webinars and
workshops with POLIS which is member of the Advisory Board of NAPCORE and has signed a
cooperation agreement to improve data provision in urban areas and regions. Indeed, due to its wide
network of involved communities, the association is an ideal partner for the organization of joint
sharing workshops with the regions and cities concerned by the cycling data provision.

5. Webinar POLIS - 11 April 2024
“PEDALLING TOWARDS CYCLING DATA STANDARDISATION”
Organisers: Laura Babio, Andréia Lopes (POLIS)
Ambassadors: Cristina BURAGA (Cerema) and Mirelle PETERS (NTM)
Taskforce: Sam Pierce (CIE), Jørgen Wanscher (HTI), Tu Tho Thai (ITxPT)
Objective:
POLIS and the NAPCORE cycling ambassadors organised this first common webinar aimed to discuss
the challenges and opportunities around cycling data. The integration of cycling as a viable mode of
transportation relies heavily on data to provide comprehensive insights into cycling patterns, usage
dynamics, and infrastructure requirements. The webinar brought together the City of Copenhagen, the
European Commission ( DG Move ), the deployEMDS consortium, the Research and Development
Centre IMEC (Interuniversity Microelectronics Centre), and the mobility data company Vianova.
The timing of the webinar coincided with the signing of the European Cycling Declaration by the
European Council, the Commission, and the Parliament, which highlights the importance of cycling in
our transportation systems and the development of sustainable mobility in Europe.
 The Cycling Ambassadors presented to POLIS members their role within NAPCORE and
emphasized that improving the collection and quality of cycling data would positively impact
infrastructure quality and cyclist safety, connected modalities, new routes and secure spaces.
 An example was done with the City of Copenhagen which collects cycling data by means of
sensors, cameras or software, completed by manual counts, surveys, and traffic model.
Greater improvements could be made with access to more data from alternative sources as
Floating Bike Data (FBD), mobile applications, video streams or from private companies.
 The representative of EC DG Move highlighted the importance of collecting bicycle data at the
local, national and EU levels, including cooperation between member states and Eurostat. In
the context of the ITS directive and the implementation of National Access Points, the vision
of a common European Mobility Data Space (EMDS) enables data sharing with clear rules on
data use and access, while aims to tackle the challenges for data security and heterogeneity
(depending on stakeholders, transport modes, or data types).
 The deployEMDS project was launched to map the existing data ecosystems and to create a
decentralised technical infrastructure for urban mobility use cases. The project connected the
Network of Followers Cities across Europe, as well the private sector to foster collaboration
and develop scale innovation solutions. For example, the Sofia use case aims to improve
cycling conditions in an urban environment, by integrating real-time data for shared bicycles
to optimise the duration and cost of travellers' trips while reducing the carbon footprints.
 IMEC is one of the partners of MegaBITS project, working to gather in-depth information on
interoperability aspects of the Floating Bicycle Data (FBD), which include raw positioning from
GNSS, but also aggregated or pre-processed data (routes, stops, seeds) and functional data of
bikes (battery status). The challenges and needs of stakeholders are different depending on
each stage of the processing from data collection to analysis and insight diffusion:
o the “hidden gems” at the beginning of the pipeline, as multimodal route planners and
bike manufacturers, lack a business model for its sale;
o the “seekers”, as cargo bike operators and policymakers, seek data for insights;
o the “intermediaries” include in-bike tech providers, IT and data companies and “allrounders”, like smartphone applications for cycling intelligence.

The ability to share data depends on one's pipeline position, but the first reason is governance
and privacy concerns. Standardisation emerges as a potential solution, favoured by
stakeholders in the insights sector, but less so by those in data collection due to their property.
The stakeholders such as European bodies (NAPCORE, CIE, EIT, CE), national bodies (NAPs,
NDW), as well as standardisation bodies (MobilityData, CEN) and smartphone applications
for cycling ITS developers should be more involved in defining new FBD standards.
 According the tech company Vianova, most mobility use cases can be sustained by anonymised
and aggregated data. Vianova succeeded in producing FBD following the prevalent data
standards from the shared mobility industry, as GBFS which is a real-time data specification
that describes the current status of a mobility system. GBFS provides a common language to
share services available to travellers, and includes information about all types of vehicles,
stations, or pricing. The primary use case for GBFS data is to communicate where vehicles are
and which ones can be rented. As data from GBFS is publicly accessible, it can be used by MaaS
applications. However, GBFS does not provide a wide variety of data (e.g. no precise data on
the origin/destination of trips), which calls for another standard of data collection.
The development of shared mobility and dockless systems led to the use of another type of
data standard, the Mobility Data Specification (MDS), which standardises communication and
data-sharing between cities and private mobility providers for e-scooters and bikes, natively
equipped with GPS or GNSS which operate in a standardised format. There is then possible to
build the right metrics as computation of trips, origin/destination pairs, distance, duration and
count of trips per road segment. MDS also has disadvantages, as the bias created on the types
of trips and the uneven distribution of data among cities, since shared data are not available.
Insights and relevant points raised from the discussion (problems vs. solutions):
 Traditional data collection and analysis approaches, shaped by motorised traffic patterns, may
not fully capture the complexities of non-motorised modes like cycling.
 The adoption of new data sources poses technical challenges and infrastructure requirements
for cities, often accompanied by significant costs.
 Private data owners often prioritise collaborations with more lucrative partners over cities,
leading to limited data accessibility for urban planning and management.
 Adapting attributes, protocols, and methodologies to these modes might be needed for
meaningful insights, and open collaboration will make this happen in a harmonised way.
 Collective data purchasing initiatives, especially beneficial for smaller cities, can amplify their
influence in the data marketplace, enabling access to valuable insights.
 Enhanced collaboration between different entities could incentivise to share data, helping to
bridge the gap between private interests and public needs.
Conclusion: A roadmap of the NAPCORE cycling community
The NAPCORE cycling ambassadors put forward the need to harmonise standards for cycling data, as
standards exist for certain domains but are not the same for all countries, and they need to be adapted
to use cases where standardisation would be an added value.
 This should be done with the cooperation of cities and regions and through workshops and
conferences with NAPCORE, POLIS and associated entities.
The goal is to bridge the gap between the different stakeholders that are relevant to the cycling data
community by focusing on the needs, challenges and desires of cities and regions while exploring the
opportunities that data can bring to cycling and more sustainable and inclusive mobility systems.
This will not be a simple exercise, as many steps lay ahead of us, but by looking locally at the
specificities of different cities and regions, we can build a comprehensive European scenario for cycling
data that can accommodate the different advancements and paces.

6. Meeting POLIS - Bike data workshop - 22 May 2024 (Utrecht)
“WHAT ARE THE NEEDS AND REQUIREMENTS OF CITIES ON CYCLING DATA?”
Organisers: Laura Babio, Andréia Lopes (POLIS)
Ambassadors: Cristina BURAGA (Cerema) and Mirelle PETERS (NTM)
Taskforce: Tu Tho Thai (ITxPT)
Objective:
The meeting kicked off with a workshop on bike data organised jointly by the Active Travel and Traffic
Efficiency POLIS Working Groups in collaboration with NAPCORE, represented at the meeting by the
Nationaal Dataportaal Wegverkeer (NDW), the Dutch National Access Point, and POLIS member
Cerema.
The workshop aimed to gather the needs and requirements of cities on cycling data: what type of
information is needed, and for which purpose should the data be used? (see Questions below)
The participants initially identified the ten most important questions encompassing various aspects of
cycling information, including infrastructure, cycling habits, modal shift, and multimodality. The main
themes selected were the trip purposes (commuting, leisure), the preferred destinations and roads,
the peak times management, the average speed, the multimodal mobility and the safety.
For each of these themes, the members were invited to think about specific use cases. The safety topic
generated many questions, such as identifying the most accidental spots, improving infrastructure,
and improving crossing safety.
Regarding multimodal traffic management, the main questions raised were about cyclists’
prioritisation of traffic lights, the location of multimodal hubs, and improving traffic modelling. The
participants were then asked to consider the required datasets to answer these questions.
The most needed datasets mentioned were infrastructure data, data on weather conditions, crash
data, user data (counting data, mobile data and behaviour surveys) and floating bike data.
Everybody agreed that a special focus should be placed on the quality, latency, privacy, and
disaggregation of this data to ensure these datasets produce relevant analysis.

Open questions:
Prioritize questions:
INFRASTRUCTURE
1. What are the cyclists’ preferred routes in my city/region?
2. Which are the safest cycling routes?
3. Where do we have cycling traffic jams?
4. What is the perceived safety of our cycling infrastructure?
5. Where do we have the most extreme rating of safety for our cycling infrastructure?
6. What kind of cycling infrastructure is required in my city/region?
7. How many cycling parking areas do we have?
8. Do we have crossroads/nodes with longer waiting times or delays for cyclists?
CYCLING HABITS
