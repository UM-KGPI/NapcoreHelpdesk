Abstract
This document describes a support system (named ENBOSS – European National Body Support
System) that allows for Service Providers and Data Providers to submit self-declarations in a central
system in case their service or data is covering multiple countries. In addition, this system is designed
to support the National Bodies in coordinating a joint compliance assessment of randomly selected
self-declarations. This approach is meant to decrease the effort needed on both sides – the providers
and the National Bodies.

Introduction
Background of the European National Bodies (NBs) Cooperation
The National Bodies (NBs) across various EU Member States often face similar challenges when
implementing the ITS Directive and its Delegated Regulations. Tackling these issues individually, in
isolation from each other, has led to inconsistencies in interpretation and redundancy in efforts. To
overcome this, a cooperative framework among NBs has been established within NAPCORE, allowing
them to address common challenges together, share expertise, and create harmonized solutions.
This collaborative effort allows NBs to benefit from the unique expertise of their peers across Europe.
For example, certain NBs may have stronger legal knowledge, while others may excel in technical
aspects or have extensive networks within the sector. By pooling these competencie s, the NBs can
develop more robust solutions. Additionally, countries that are in the process of establishing a National
Body or do not yet have one in place can still contribute to and benefit from this cooperation,
enhancing the knowledge base and creating a more integrated European approach.
Furthermore, many stakeholders affected by the Delegated Regulations are multinational companies.
By working together, NBs not only gain more credibility but also strengthen their negotiating power,
enabling them to engage effectively with these large mult inational entities. Finally, collaboration
among NBs reduces duplication of efforts, leading to more efficient and consistent compliance
assessments (CAs), information sharing, and stakeholder engagement.
Importance of Cooperation for Multinational Collaboration
The challenges faced by National Bodies extend beyond borders, especially when dealing with
multinational organizations. These companies operate across multiple Member States, which often
means they have to comply with various interpretations of EU regulations. This inconsistency creates
inefficiencies both for the multinational entities and for the National Bodies. Through a cooperative
framework, NBs can align their processe s and interpretations, ensuring that multinational
organizations face a uniform set of requirements across Europe.
By coordinating their efforts, NBs can also strengthen their position when engaging with multinational
stakeholders, ensuring that compliance assessments are more credible and less burdensome both for
the multinational stakeholders and the National Bodies . Furthermore, this strengthens the ability of
NBs to effectively implement the Delegated Regulations across Europe in a consistent and efficient
manner.
Objectives and structure of this report
In this report, the scope of cooperative work amongst the NBs is widened to cover a common technical
tool that supports the National Bodies, especially when it comes to dealing with multi-national data or
service providers. The objective is to describe a possible supporting tool and process in a prototypical
manner to show how a tool and process could look like, what it can cover, where it helps, and what
issues remain or are new.
After this introduction, in the second chapter, the goals of the cooperation of the NBs on a European
level are further described and options on the level of cooperation are given. The next chapter is
dedicated to describing a technical tool and process: the “ENBOSS” – a European National Body
Support System for multi-national compliance assessment. This system has been divided into modules,

Goals of European National Bodies cooperation
The cooperation among NBs aims to achieve several goals, each of which focuses on improving
efficiency, harmonization, and collaboration.
Regular Discussion on Shared Challenges
One of the core goals of this cooperation is to provide a forum where NBs can regularly discuss and
address shared questions regarding their tasks, such as compliance assessments (CAs), stakeholder
management, and the interpretation of Delegated Regulations. By meeting regularly, NBs can address
common concerns, including:
• Compliance Assessment (CA) Questions: For example, how to interpret specific aspects of the
regulations, such as restrictive licensing or random inspections.
• Informing Stakeholders : Sharing best practices for how to communicate effectively with
stakeholders affected by the regulations.
• Stakeholder List Management : Coordinating approaches to maintaining and updating
stakeholder lists.
By discussing these topics collectively, NBs can benefit in several ways:
• Shared Expertise: Each NB can contribute knowledge from their area of expertise, whether
legal, technical, or operational, ensuring that all NBs are better equipped to handle complex
challenges.
• Harmonized Interpretations : By working together, NBs can formulate consistent
interpretations of the Delegated Regulations, ensuring that companies face the same
standards across Europe.
• Efficient Use of Resources : Rather than solving the same problems independently, NBs can
save time and resources by working together on shared issues.
Joint Communication with EU-Level Sector Federations
Many of the stakeholders affected by the Delegated Regulations are multinational organizations
represented by sector -specific federations at the EU level. Examples include the European Parking
Association (EPA), ACRISS (representing car rental companies), MOVE-EU (for shared mobility), and
ChargeUp Europe (for electric vehicle charging infrastructure). By coordinating their communication
and engagement with these federations, NBs can:
• Present a unified message regarding the obligations of these multinationals under the
Delegated Regulations.
• Increase their impact and credibility when informing stakeholders about regulatory
requirements.
• Explore possible collaborative efforts, such as joint initiatives or standardized approaches
across sectors.

Another important goal of cooperation is to streamline compliance assessments for multinational
organizations. These organizations often operate across multiple Member States, which can lead to
fragmented and inconsistent compliance processes. Through collaboration, NBs can:
• Address Multinationals Together : By coordinating their efforts, NBs can engage with
multinational organizations as a single entity, simplifying the process for these companies and
improving consistency.
• Share CA Results : Where applicable, NBs can mutually recognize the results of compliance
assessments carried out by other Member States, reducing duplication and inconsistencies.
• Divide Workload: For complex cases involving multinationals, certain compliance assessment
tasks can be taken up by a multi -national support system (like the one described below) ,
allowing for a more efficient and resource-effective approach.
This collaborative approach will not only reduce the workload for individual NBs but also ensure that
the compliance process is more credible and uniform across the EU. It will also enhance the NBs' ability
to engage with multinational organizations in a meaningful way.
Progress Towards Formal Collaboration
The current cooperation among NBs can serve as a stepping stone towards more formalized structures,
such as the potential future establishment of a European body under the NAPCORE framework. While
this collaboration is currently informal, it is important to consider future opportunities for more formal
agreements and structures that will further enhance the ability of NBs to work together.
How could such cooperation look like? For effective cooperation among National Bodies (NBs) in
Europe, certain organizational structures, resources, and platforms need to be in place. These
elements will enable smooth collaboration, consistent communication, and efficient handling of
multinational tasks, such as compliance assessments (CAs). Below are key component s required to
ensure the success of such a cooperative framework:
1. Establishment of a Coordinating Body
A central coordinating body would be responsible for organizing and moderating meetings,
setting agendas, recording meeting notes, and ensuring that tasks are distributed efficiently.
This role can be shared on a rotational basis between different NBs or divided among two or
more bodies to balance responsibilities. The coordinating body would act as the backbone of
the collaboration, ensuring that all activities are well-structured and progressing as planned.
→ NAPCORE can (e.g. with a dedicated Working Group) form an organisational framework
for this National Body coordinating body.
2. Regular Meetings
To ensure consistent progress and open communication, regular meetings should be
scheduled, such as every six months. These meetings provide an opportunity for NBs to discuss
ongoing challenges, share results of compliance assessments, and coordinate future actions.
The regularity of these meetings will help maintain momentum and ensure that no issues are
left unresolved for too long.
→ NAPCORE’s current WG5 has already formed an organisational framework for this National
Body coordinating body.
3. A Secure Collaboration Platform
The cooperation will require a secure platform for sharing information and documents,
enabling continuous collaboration between meetings. This platform could host forums or chat

4. Formation of Specialized Working Groups
For specific issues or tasks, temporary working groups can be established to focus on
particular challenges or topics (e.g., sector -specific compliance assessments). Each working

5. Minimum Legal Framework and Agreements
For any formal collaboration on compliance assessments or other shared tasks, a legal
framework must be established. This framework will formalize the cooperation, ensuring that
all NBs commit to their roles and responsibilities. In addition, GDPR-compliant agreements
must be made to allow the sharing of contact information and CA results between NBs. These
agreements will provide the legal basis for cross -border data sharing, ensuring that all
collaborative efforts comply with European privacy regulations.
6. Allocation of Sufficient Resources
Each Member State must commit to allocating sufficient resources—both in terms of funding
and working hours—so that their NB can actively participate in the cooperation. The benefits
of this collaboration, such as reduced workloads for individual NBs and improved efficiency ,
will justify the investment. Adequate funding will also be required to support the role of the
coordinating body and to maintain the collaboration platform. Financial support for these
activities could be derived from shared national funding mechanisms or through co-funding
arrangements with European entities (e.g. NAPCORE).
By ensuring these components are in place, the cooperation among National Bodies can move towards
a more formal and structured collaboration, benefiting all participating countries and stakeholders. It
will allow for a more efficient, transparent, and cons istent approach to managing multinational
compliance assessments and other shared regulatory tasks.

Challenge
Currently, multinational organizations are required to submit a self-declaration (SD) (upon request) in
each Member State where they operate. This fragmented approach results in inefficiencies for both
the organizations and the National Bodies (NBs). A multinational company can, in theory, be asked to
submit up to 30 separate self-declarations for a single Delegated Regulation, depending on how many
Member States they operate in.
Once submitted, these organizations may also be selected for compliance assessments in multiple
Member States through random selection processes. This not only places a significant administrative
burden on the companies but also introduces inefficiencies for the NBs, which must process and assess
the same company multiple times in different countries.
Moreover, this decentralized system can lead to conflicting outcomes. A multinational organization
may be deemed compliant by one NB, while another NB reviewing the same dataset may come to a

Proposed Solution: The ENBOSS System
As a potential future approach to address inefficiencies and inconsistencies in the current system,
NAPCORE WG5 has envisioned a unified solution. This vision seeks to simplify the submission of self -
declarations (SDs) for multinational organizations while decreasing the administrative workload and
assessment demands on National Bodies (NBs). We propose the European National Body Support
System (ENBOSS) as a conceptual digital platform designed to support the submission, management,
and assessment of self-declarations on a multinational scale.
The vision of ENBOSS provides a solution that a llows multinationals to submit one SD , preferably
digitally, which is valid for all Member States. This would help motivate the multinationals to submit
SDs and thus increase the number of submitted SDs. Additionally, the tool could assist the NBs in multinational compliance assessments, since these international SDs could be assessed at a multinational
level, leading to one harmonized outcome for the CA and decreasing the workload for the individual
NBs. On top of that ENBOSS can also be in charge of the random selection process and supervise the
multinational CA (carried out by one or more NB).
In the next sub chapters a detailed description of the ENBOSS-solution is given. The process is divided
in different modules (the red boxes), and for each module the goals, the requirements, the process
and the outputs as well as the identified potential issues are described. The overall picture on page 11
can be hard to read, but in each sub chapter one module is enhanced to a readable size.
General description and components of ENBOSS
ENBOSS (European National BOdy Support System) is a centralized platform that manages the end-toend workflow for self-declaration and compliance assessments, providing a standardized mechanism
for data providers, National Bodies, and NAPCORE to communicat e and process relevant documents.
It ensures consistency across participating bodies by handling submissions of self-declarations, random
selection for compliance assessments, document management, and final compliance reporting.
Core Components
• Web Interface for Self-Declaration (SD) Submission
- A front-end that allows data providers to log in, submit self -declarations, and attach
any required documentation.
• API for Data Transfer (NAP / NB systems to ENBOSS)
- A programmatic interface enabling National Access Points and National Bodies to send
or retrieve self-declaration data directly to ENBOSS.
• Secure Database
- A data store where all self -declaration records, user information, metadata, and
compliance information are securely held.
• Document Management System

• The SD templates created by NAPCORE can be the basis for the digital SD-forms.
- The submitter must be able to declare for which countries it is registering data and/or
services and be informed that the necessary information will be shared with the
different European NBs/CAs.
- It should not be possible to submit a non -compliant SD (so option “No , I will not
comply”) should not be possible.
- The form should also contain a direct the link to the registered dataset(s) and/or
service(s).
- A unique identification connecting SD forms and NAP registered organisations is
needed.
• A first example of a digital SD-form can be found in Annex I. No signature will be required for
the digital SD as this would complicate the procedure very much (no EU -wide easy to
implement digital signature solution exists).
• The NAP-operators will need to investigate how a digital SD-form can technically be integrated
in their NAP. The digital SD-form should not be an external form (e.g. Microsoft forms, Googleforms).

Once the self-declaration is submitted in ENBOSS, it will undergo an automatic completeness check.
From there on, two options are possible:
1. The SD requires improvements and/or additional information to be completed. This means
that NAPCORE will request improvements of the SD via ENBOSS. The data provider shall make
the required adaptations and re -submit its international self -declaration to ENBOSS. Frow
there on it will have to undergo a new automatic completeness check.
2. The SD does not require improvements . In this situation the SD is stored and may now be
selected for a random selection for a compliance assessment.

• The service or data provider submits the filled in AD to ENBOSS and/or makes the necessary

Description of the simulated process – Module 4
Goal of this module
The objective of this module is to initiate a process for an in-depth compliance assessment (= content
assessment) in an international context. The module begins by selecting which National Bodies (NBs)
will perform the compliance assessment. Once the NBs are selected, the responsibility for assessing
specific self-declarations (SDs) is formally assigned to them. The designated NBs then carry out the
harmonized compliance assessments by verifying the data's compliance with the Delegated
Regulations, ensuring accuracy, timeliness, and completeness, and identifying any potential
discrepancies. Finally, ENBOSS consolidates the assessment results and forwards them to the European
NB Group for review.
Requirements and input(s)
• Digitally submitted SDs available in the ENBOSS
• Information and result stored in ENBOSS from the previous Module (3)
• Knowledge of the compliance assessment process as defined by NAPCORE
• Harmonized template for recording results
• European NB Group (for reviewing results)
Process description
Agree on which National Bodies perform compliance assessment (4.1):
All NBs of all affected countries are invited to carry out the CA on a voluntary basis. Agreement
on National Bodies (NBs) on a voluntary basis to conduct in-depth compliance assessments of
SDs previously flagged in Module 3. The selected NBs are informed, and their roles and
responsibilities for assessing the SDs are confirmed. Once the selection is complete, the
process moves to the next phase.
Assign responsible National Bodies for in-depth compliance assessment (4.2):
ENBOSS formally assigns the selected NBs to the specific SDs . Each NB has access to the
necessary documents such as the SD, the ADs and the most recent versions of the harmonized
templates and methodological guidelines . This step ensures that the NBs are fully equipped
and informed about their tasks. Each assigned NB carries out the CA focusing on the data
regarding their MS(s)

Process description
Module 5 outlines a structured process for handling the outcomes of compliance assessments (CA)
related to data/service providers. Here is a step-by-step overview of the process:
Outcome Reporting (5.1): After performing the CA process, ENBOSS receives a report from the chosen
NBs about the results of CA and records the outcome. This overall result is stored in the ENBOSS
system.
Result – “compliant” (5.2): If the outcome is positive (“compliant"), ENBOSS takes the initiative to
communicate this positive feedback to the data and/or service provider. This reinforces compliance
and good practices.
Resubmission (5.3): The data and/or service provider need to resubmit or validate their data and SD(s)
in a reasonable timeframe.
Result – “non-compliant” (5.4): If the outcome is negative (“non-compliant”), ENBOSS communicates
the negative feedback to the data and/or service provider and requests them to make the necessary
corrections and/or submit additional documentation to ENBOSS.
Submission of Improvements (5.5): In response to the improvement request, the data and/or service
provider receives the communication and, within a reasonable timeframe, submits updated
documents and improvements to ENBOSS.
Submission of Changes (5.6): Finally, the data and/or service provider communicates the changes
made regarding their data, metadata, and documentation back to ENBOSS. This confirms that the
feedback has been acted upon and allows the selected NBs to verify the improvements.

Annex 2: Requirements from the perspective of National Bodies for semiautomated support tools or the electronic implementation of CA processes
and forms
Introduction
This document serves as an annex to M5.10 - Multi-National Compliance Assessment and defines the
requirements for the development of a semi -automated system to support a more eff icient,
transparent, and interoperable process for Compliance Assessment (CA) and the validation of Self-
Declarations (SD) within NAPCORE.
The main goal of this system is to reduce the administrative burden on National Bodies (NB), ensure
harmonization of the compliance assessment process across EU Member States , and enhance
coordination between NB and ENBOSS. The system will combine automated and digital processes –
performing automated validation and selection of SDs for inspections, while key decision -making
remains with NB.
As next step in system design, a structured questionnaire will be developed, which will be distributed
among NB within NAPCORE. This questionnaire will be used to validate requirements, determine
functionality priorities, and collect feedback on national specifics of Compliance Assessment (CA)
process.
Objectives and Expected Benefits of the System
Key objectives of the semi-automated system:
• Simplification of SD submission and management – a unified environment for SD
registration and metadata recording.
• Automated validation of SD – the system performs basic format and structure validations.
• Support for NB in Compliance Assessment – digital tools for manual validation, inspections,
and reporting.
• Harmonization of the CA process across NB – sharing of inspection results and Compliance
Reports among Member States.
• Integration with NAP and ENBOSS – linking to external platforms for interoperability.
Benefits for key stakeholders:
• For NB – reduced administrative burden, faster decision-making, improved oversight of SDs
across the EU.
• For Data Holders – clearer SD requirements, a simpler submission and validation process.
• For the European Commission (EC) – a unified Compliance Assessment system across the EU,
better compliance monitoring.

Key Functional Requirements of the System
The semi-automated system could be designed to support the following functions, divided into key
phases of the CA process.
Difference Between Digital and Automated Functionality
• Digital functionality – Processes requiring active user interaction (e.g., login, manual SD
validation, escalation of decisions).
• Automated functionality – Processes executed without user intervention, based on
predefined rules (e.g., formal validation of SDs, automated selection of inspections).
• Manual activities – Actions performed directly by NB, in cases where automated analysis is
insufficient (e.g., SD content review, escalation of inspections).

Distribution and Response Collection
• The questionnaire will be distributed to all participating NB via the established NAPCORE
communication channels.
• Responses will be collected and analyzed to identify common pain points and nationalspecific requirements.
• A synthesis report will be created summarizing key findings.
Synchronization with deliverables from Other Work Packages (WP2, WP4, etc.)
Objective: Ensure that the semi-automated system is aligned with the broader objectives and
technological developments of the NAPCORE project.
Collaboration with WP2 (Harmonization of NAPs and Data Exchange)
• Align the system requirements with WP2's efforts to harmonize National Access Points
(NAPs).
• Ensure that SD metadata structures are compatible with the standardized formats used in
NAPs.
• Validate data exchange mechanisms between NB, NAP, and ENBOSS.
Integration with WP4 (Data Quality and Monitoring)
• Incorporate WP4 findings related to data validation, quality control, and monitoring.
• Ensure that the semi-automated system supports automatic verification of SD metadata and
enables NB to track the quality of compliance over time.
• Define standardized metrics for evaluating SD compliance, to be used in Compliance
Reports.
Technical Coordination with WP5.9 (NB Reference Architecture)
• Ensure that the system architecture follows the reference framework developed in WP5.9.
• Validate the system’s modular approach so that it can evolve alongside other NAPCORE
technological developments.
• Ensure that interoperability requirements for multi-national CA processes are fully
addressed.
Technical Feasibility Assessment and Drafting the System Specification
Objective: Define the technical architecture and key implementation milestones based on the
collected requirements.
System Functional Specification
• Define system workflows for SD validation, Compliance Assessment, and Reporting.
• Establish data governance principles to ensure NB retain control over compliance processes.
• Define the rules for automatic validation and manual verification workflows.
API and Data Integration Model
• Define API endpoints for NB interaction with NAP, ENBOSS, and the Compliance
Assessment system.
