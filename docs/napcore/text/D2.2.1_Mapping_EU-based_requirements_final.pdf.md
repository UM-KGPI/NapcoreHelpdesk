Abstract
This report, "Mapping of the EU-based requirements, governance and rules to implement MMTIS DR",
constitutes a deliverable of the NAPCORE project, a Programme Support Action funded under the
Connecting Europe Facility (CEF). It aims to facilitate EU -wide coordination of National Access Points
(NAPs) for the harmonized implementation of European spe cifications on the ITS Directive
(2010/40/EU), particularly focusing on Multimodal Travel Information Services (MMTIS).

Within the framework of NAPCORE Task 2.2, the current report presents:
1. The challenges identified for MMTIS data provision at NAPs based on discussions and
experiences from countries participating in this task, and information retrieved from the
previous phase of NAPCORE.
2. The development of an Excel database that maps MMTIS DR Annex data categories to specific
types of services. This database serves as a guide for NAPs and National Bodies to request
harmonized information from data holders across different countries.
3. Practical documentation of best practices from the OpRa standard for historical data, and the
implementation of the Finnish , Germany (Baden – Württemberg), and Norwegian NAPs
including their governance and approach to face the challenges identified on task 2.2.
This report will be complemented by further best practices in other countries and be the foundation
for the second deliverable of task 2.2 ‘Roadmap and recommendations to increase MMTIS data
provision on the NAPs demonstrated by best practices’, due December 2026.

1. Introduction
This report is the first outcome of the Task 2.2, Multimodal Data Availability on the NAPs of NAPCORE
(National Access Point Coordination Organisation for Europe), a technical assistance Action - funded
under the Connecting Europe Facility (CEF) 1. NAPCORE gathers public authorities of all EU members
states and aims at facilitating EU wide coordination of NAPs (National Access Points) for the
harmonisation of the implementation of the European specifications adopted in the framework of the
ITS Directive (Directive 2010/40/EU).
UITP – the International Association of Public Transport - is a partner of NAPCORE for what relates to
Multimodal Travel Information Services Delegated Regulation (MMTIS)2 (EU) 2017/1926 and modified
by Comission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/490 . In this role, UITP leads the development of the
“Mapping of the EU -based requirements, governance and rules to implement MMTIS DR” which is
addressed in this report, and carried out within Working Group 2 on Multimodal Mobility.

The first part of this task has two parts:

categories3, with tailored data categories for different modes and services . This Excel file will
be supplemented by a concise implementation report and Frequently Asked Questions.
2. Stakeholder challenge analysis: presented in this report, analysis of challenges identified by
stakeholders, gathered through meetings in the second half of 2025 and findings from
NAPCORE’s first phase.

2.2. Challenges identified
2.2.1. Historic data
Purpose, retention, and usability. Clarification is needed on whether archiving previously uploaded
data is sufficient for MMTIS DR Data category 1.4, or if more comprehensive solutions are required.
Data category 1.4 ( historic and observed data) supports trip planning, delay calculations, and
passenger compensation claims, with retention periods aligning with compensation deadlines. For
infrastructure planning and machine learning -based predictions, datasets should cover at least one
year (preferably 3-5) to account for seasonal and short-term effects.
The revised MMTIS DR mandates sharing historic and observed data, particularly for linking delays and
cancellations to passenger rights. However, it remains unclear how historic data can effectively
support calculations for trip plans, fuel consumption, and environmental factors.
The OpRa (Operating Raw Data and Statistics Exchange) standard, led by Fabrizio Arneodo was
presented within the task to understand its role on historic data, the key highlights of that presentation
are included on this report.

2.2.2. Cross-border data
It remains unclear whether cross-border data should be managed by one country, both, or another
entity. Ensuring data provision, quality, and exchange is key for cross-border planning, but it is tied to
data reliability, standardization, and proper formatting. NeTEx (Network Timetable Exchange) enables
integrated cross-border datasets, but differing national profiles complicates compatibility.
The overarching goal is to ensure cross -border data meets standards to support multimodal travel
information services effectively. A unified NeTEx profile is being developed in NAPCORE task 4.3. Task
2.2 will focus on best practices and facilitators to ensure cross-border data compatibility.
2.2.3. Data provision
Lack of awareness and perceived benefits among smaller stakeholders (e.g., shared mobility services),
about the benefits of registering data on NAPs. Standards (e.g., NeTEx and SIRI) pose technical and
management challenges, making it difficult to convince stakeholders to provide data. Emphasising
legal obligations (MMTIS DR) and tangible benefits, such as increased data reuse and visibility is critical.
Limited resources, knowledge gaps, and reliance on third -party service s disproportionately affect
smaller players. It is proposed to share and document best practices and experiences across Member
States.
2.2.4. Data update
Datasets must be updated when changes occur. Infrastructure datasets may require yearly updates,
while public transport timetables (NeTEx) should be updated daily or in real -time (SIRI). Content
providers and data owners are the primarily responsible for ensuring reliability and accuracy.
The MMTIS Data Dictionary ( NAPCORE Task 5.3) may provide guidance, but best practices for update
frequencies tailored to data types are required. Proved experiences indicating roles, and dataflow can
contribute to solve it.
Automation, clear standards and profiles could reduce the burden on data providers, particularly local
authorities and operators.
2.2.5. Data availability
Ensuring data availability for journey planners and other multimodal services was identified as a
challenge. This includes providing data that covers all transport modes according to the MMTIS DR and
addressing gaps in definitions, such as what data must be registered by different transport types. This
was tackled with an Excel database indic ating the data categories applicable per type of
service/transport mode.
Privacy concerns also arise, particularly for on -demand transport, where live vehicle locations should
not be shared during use, though parking availability and service geofences should be visible. For
personal transport, ride -sharing data should be includ ed, while for occupancy data, clarity is needed
on whether real -time or forecasted data should be shared and at what level of detail. Barrier -free
access points and routes must also be considered.
2.2.6. Data categories
Definitions and boundaries between planned and real -time data in rail and public transport remain
unclear. There is a n alignment need between standards like DATEX -II and NeTEx to improve
interoperability. The Level of Service 1.4 MMTIS requirements remain unclear, and data categories
applicable to shared mobility face format challenges due to complexity.

4. OpRa
The Operating Raw Data and Statistics Exchange (OpRa) is a standardization initiative, developed under
the CEN/TC 278 committee within Working Group 3, focused on public transport standards. Presented
on February 2026 by Fabrizio Arneodo, the OpRa Coordinator, as part of NAPCORE T2.2.
4.1. The need for OpRa
OpRa fills a gap in public transport data standards. While NeTEx handles planned data and SIRI
manages real-time information, there was no standardized way to exchange operational raw data —
the observed events occur ring during service execution. This gap made it difficult for authorities and
operators to monitor performance, compare metrics across systems, or fulfil regulatory requirements

4.2. Technical and standardization approach
OpRa initiative began as a CEN Technical Report in 2019 and evolved into OpRa+ project in 2023,
funded by the European Commission (EC) to develop a Technical Specification (TS). OpRa will become
mandatory for EU Member States once it is published as a CEN Technical Specification and integrated
into the MMTIS Delegated Regulation.
Public transport operators (PTOs) and authorities (PTAs) have struggled with inconsistent data formats
for performance monitoring. Without a common structure, comparing KPIs across operators or regions
was challenging. OpRa defines a standardized way to represent operational raw data, including
timestamps, vehicle positions, delays, and dwell times, and linking them to planned data (e.g., NeTEx).
The primary motivation is to enable quantitative performance monitoring that supports:
• Performance-based contracts, where authorities need verifiable data to assess operator
compliance.
• Regulatory audits, such as those conducted by national or regional transport agencies.
• Internal improvement processes, where operators use historical data to identify inefficiencies.
• Data sharing in Mobility Data Spaces, facilitating interoperability with other systems like
Automatic Vehicle Location/Monitoring (AVL/AVM), ticketing, and Mobility-as-a-Service
(MaaS) platforms.
OpRa is designed to be agnostic to the underlying systems—whether data comes from onboard units,
control centres, or ticketing systems, it can be structured consistently for exchange and analysis.
OpRa is compliant with Transmodel, and its Part 8 (Operations Monitoring and Control), it reuses
structures from NeTEx and SIRI for its XML Schema (XSD). This approach ensures that OpRa integrates
seamlessly with existing standards while adding the missing operational data layer. The UML (Unified
Modeling Language) modelling for OpRa is done within Transmodel Part 8, and the physical exchange
format leverages NeTEx’s artifacts (e.g., <JourneyPatternRef>, <DatedVehicleJourneyRef>). The reuse
of existing schemas minimizes implementation overhead for systems using NeTEx and/or SIRI.

One innovative aspect of OpRa is its contextual indicators framework. This feature addresses how to
exchange non -standard KPIs (e.g., local or contractual metrics) without modifying the standard.
Contextual indicators act as a placeholder within OpRa’s structure, allowing producer -consumer pairs
to exchange custom metrics that are not part of the EU -standardized set. For example, a city might
require vehicle cleanliness scores, or a national authority might track fuel consumption rates.
To implement contextual indicators, two elements must be defined:
1. ID Tags: Unique identifiers for each custom indicator (e.g., VehicleCleanlinessClassification).
2. Regulation Context: A reference to the governing document that mandates the indicator.
Contextual indicators must reuse OpRa’s Transmodel -based reference system, enabling them to link
to standard entities like networks, journeys, or stop points. Contextual indicators could include:
employee performance on a vehicle journey, signage quality, fuel consumption or emission levels.

4.7. Adoption challenges and real-world insights
4.7.1. Italian Case Study: Early Adoption
The National Regulatory Transport Authority is incorporating OpRa principles into new transport
contracts. The authority has defined 30 KPIs for public transport contracts, many of which align with
OpRa’s scope.
In the Milan region, a public tender now explicitly requires PTOs to provide raw data using NeTEx and
SIRI Italian profiles, to enable the calculation of National Regulatory Transport Authority defined KPIs,
waiting for the OpRa official TS CEN publication and MS adoption. The tender includes detailed
annexes specifying which raw data must be provided (e.g., timestamps, vehicle positions, delays).
National NeTEx and SIRI guidelines are publicly available for system integrators, ensuring consistent
implementation. OpRa experts are supporting PTAs in drafting tenders that reference OpRa.

4.7.2. Challenges
4.7.2.1. GTFS – Understanding of Transmodel
Even though many operators prefer GTFS due to its simplicity, GTFS lacks the structural depth needed
for operational raw data (e.g., no support for duties, blocks, or detailed event logging). There is a
MobilityData GTFS -based project exploring similar con cepts, but OpRa remains the EU -standardized
solution for historical data exchange.
Many PTAs and PTOs lack expertise in Transmodel -based standards (NeTEx, SIRI, OpRa). This is being
addressed in different tasks in NAPCORE.
4.7.2.1. Data retention policies
OpRa does not prescribe retention periods, as this is governed by contracts or regulations, MMTIS
requirements vary by transport mode. In Piedmont (Italy), for example, historical data is retained for
5 years due to local regulatory requirements. Flexibility is key: producers (PTOs) and consumers (PTAs)
can agree on retention policies based on their needs and regulations. An additional webinar organised
between CEN-CENELEC and DG MOVE took place on Jun 25th 20265 to provide further clarifications.

• Regulators: Align national and regional policies with OpRa’s framework to ensure consistent

• NAPCORE and CEN/TC 278: Continue dissemination and training to accelerate adoption and

5.5. Additional modes, additional challenges
Taxi data provision and availability remains a challenge. Despite having thousands of taxi companies
registered in metadata and sharing their prices —and some larger operators sharing dispatch or sales
APIs—there are no use cases of NeTEx in taxi data.
While dispatch APIs are open, data models in the taxi sector are disperse. In publicly funded services,
various implementations use different forks of the Nordic SUTI (Standardized Utilization of Transport
Information) standard. There are concerns about forcing NeTEx compliance on the taxi sector, given
its dynamic and business-specific nature.
Benefits and use cases for applying a relatively static standard like NeTEx to highly dynamic services
(including shared cars, e-scooters, and bike-sharing schemes) need to be clarified, this remains an open
question for discussion.

and on-street parking are missing from the national system. The sector is a "wild mixture" of:

• Publicly owned (or partially publicly owned) parking companies

System
The Act on Information Services for the Transport System (Act on Finish only ) came into force on
January 1, 2026. The main goal is to establish a more unified basis for the interoperable deployment
of ITS and the utilization of transport-related information. The act addresses issues with existing laws,
including the Transport Act, and the Law concerning digital systems on roads. Key improvements are:
• Data requirements and standards implemented from the ITS Directive.
• Clear responsibility for data holders/producers to deliver data.
• Defined national responsibilities:
o Fintraffic as the national operator and coordinator.
o Traficom as the national body and authority.
Additional requirements:
• Creation of national databases for selected datasets.
• Clear scope for other ITS Directive-like national data to remain open (primarily through
digitraffic.fi).
• Enforceable with fines (administered by Traficom).

• Public transport delay data is archived for predictive modelling of future disruptions and to

management strategies.
• Real-time data (e.g., vehicle positions, parking availability) may be retained for short -term

long-term planning.
• The event management system, introduced in 2025, logs historical events (e.g., disruptions,
accidents) to improve real-time data accuracy and system resilience.

7.2. From 1869 to 2017’ railway reform
Norway’s national transport data system began in 1869 when book dealer Albert Cammermeyer
launched Norges kommunikasjoner (Norway’s Communications), a bi-weekly publication consolidating
steamboat and railway timetables. It later evolved into Rutebok for Norge (Timetable for Norway), a
quarterly phonebook-style catalog where operators were legally required to publish their timetables,
with costs calculated per millimeter of display space.
By the early 2000s, Norway faced a highly fragmented data ecosystem with:
• Nearly 400 different data sources, each using the Norwegian format in inconsistent ways.
• Manual reconciliation was required daily, as the same data field often had different
meanings across sources.
In the 2010’s 20 regions (counties) each took responsibility for public transport data in their area,
leading to the adoption of regional planning tools, which reduced the number of data sources from
~400 to ~60. However, private operators, cross-regional operators, railways, air services, and some
ferries remained as individual data sources outside the regional structure.
In 2017 the Norwegian government reformed the railway sector, splitting the state -owned NSB
(Norges Statsbaner) into separate companies:
• Bane NOR (rail management)
• Spordrift (rail maintenance)
• Mantena (rolling stock administration)
• ENTUR (information & ticketing)
This reform introduced competition in rail operations, with four railway operators serving Norway.

7.5.1. Nordic NeTEx Profile
The Nordic NeTEx profile is a living document that specifies which parts of the NeTEx standard are used
in Norway. It d efines frames, objects, fields, and values required for Nordic use cases. It e xplicitly
clarifies how data should be interpreted. It is backward-compatible, allowing for continuous expansion
and revision. Documentation available in English Nordic NeTEx Profile.
7.5.2. Nordic SIRI Profile
The Nordic SIRI profile scopes the data needed for Nordic use cases. It Defines protocols for:
• ET (Estimated Timetable): For real-time estimates. Documentation: SIRI ET.

• SX (Situation Exchange): For deviation messages. Documentation: SIRI SX.

9.1. OpRa conceptual example
OpRa models delayed and cancelled journeys (two P1 use cases for MMTIS compliance ). A fictional
example is presented below.
1. One delayed journey: A 5-minute delay caused by the late return of a vehicle from a previous
service (Event: LateReturnVehicleEvent).
2. One cancelled journey: A cancellation without replacement due to vehicle damage (Event:
ReportDamageToVehicleEvent).
OpRa’s physical exchange format reuses NeTEx artifacts, ensuring compatibility with existing systems.
The examples below demonstrate the structure:
Delayed Journey Entry. Each delayed journey is logged with:
• A reference to the journey pattern and dated vehicle journey (from NeTEx).
• The delay duration (e.g., PT5M for 5 minutes).
• The triggering event, including its type, ID, and summary.
Cancelled Journey Entry. Each cancelled journey includes:
• The journey pattern and dated vehicle journey references.
• The control action type (e.g., JourneyCancelation).
• The triggering event(s) (e.g., ReportDamageToVehicleEvent with ID eid730 and summary
"vehicle tire damage").
• A flag indicating whether a replacement journey was created (false in this case).
Statistical Aggregation. OpRa also supports statistical summaries of delays and cancellations, such as:
• Duration intervals (e.g., 0–10 minutes, 10–20 minutes) with counts and percentages.
• Cancellation counts with percentages of total journeys.
The statistical summary for this example and period showed and saved on OpRa:
• 72 journeys delayed by <10 minutes (97.3% of total).
• 2 journeys delayed by 10–20 minutes (2.7% of total).
• 3 total cancellations (0.04% of total journeys).

For providers using non -standard formats, MobiData BW develops custom converters to map their

who may lack the resources to adopt standardized formats. All developed tools (e.g., converters, APIs)
are published on GitHub.
To identify which datasets are most used, MobiData BW uses analytics tools within its API endpoints
and data catalog. Metrics such as API call frequency, download counts, and user feedback help
prioritize data maintenance and expansion efforts. For example, if parking data is frequently accessed
by developers, MobiData BW may prioritize its updates or coverage. Additionally, the platform
conducts regular surveys with data consumers to gauge satisfaction and needs.
