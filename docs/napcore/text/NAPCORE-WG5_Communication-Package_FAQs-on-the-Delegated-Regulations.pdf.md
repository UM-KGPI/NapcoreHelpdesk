1. General questions
1.1. What is a NAP? Why do I need to provide data on the NAPs?
A National Access Point (NAP) is a digital interface set up by a Member State that constitutes a single
point of access to transport data required by the ITS directive . NAPs are set up to facilitate access,
easy exchange and reuse of transport data in Europe, to help support the provision of EU -wide
interoperable travel and traffic services to end users.
The legal basis for the creation of the National Access Points are the Delegated Regulations of the ITS
Directive. Every Member State of the European Union is required to create a NAP for the different
Delegated Regulations. An overview of the existing NAPs can be found through this link.

1.2. What are intelligent transport systems (ITS)?
‘Intelligent Transport Systems’ or ‘ITS’ means systems in which information and communication
technologies are applied in the field of road transport, including infrastructure, vehicles and users, and
in traffic/mobility management, as well as for interfac es with other modes of transport. ITS are
advanced applications that provide innovative services in transport modes and traffic management.
With ITS, users can be better informed which contributes to a safer, more coordinated and smarter
use of transport modes and networks.
1.3. What is the National Body/Competent Authority for mobility data?
A National Body (NB)/Competent Authority (CA) typically refers to an organisation, institution, or entity
that operates at a national level. These bodies are established by the government or recognised by
authorities to oversee, manage, regulate, or represent various aspects of national affairs. They must
be impartial and independent.
In the legal framework of the ITS Directive 2010/40/EU and related Delegated Regulations the main
task of a National Body/Competent Authority is to assess whether data holders and service providers
comply with the requirements set out in the Delegated Regu lations, including requirements on data
quality (compliance assessment).
1.4. What is the relation between a NAP and a National Body/ Competent Authority
The (meta-) data required as per the Delegated Regulations is collected on the National Access Points
(NAPs). National Bodies/Competent Authorities are generally concerned with assessing the description
of the data accessible on the NAP by carrying out compliance assessments on a regular basis.

1.5. What are the benefits of sharing data on the NAP
− Your data becomes visible and easily findable for a broader group of users across Europe and
beyond.
− Providing data on the NAPs can encourage the provision of new mobility services, i.e. services
for climate-friendly mobility solutions (MaaS, etc).
− Your data can be reused easily, and this opens new business opportunities.
− The NAP is one centralised source of data where specific standards are used, and certain levels
of quality are ensured.
− The NAPs grant equal access to data re-users and ensure non-discrimination.
− Cumulative effects (the more data/service providers and users the NAPs already have, the
more attractive it is for other data/service providers and users to join).
− Your data may be verified by the NB upon random inspection, so you will receive detailed
feedback that can be useful to improve your data.
− Your Corporate Social Responsibility can be enriched with your contribution to increased
safety and to the reduction of CO2-emissions across the European Road Network.
− By registering on the NAPs, you comply with the Delegated Regulations (EU).
1.6. Self-declarations
1.6.1. What is a declaration of compliance, also called self-declaration (SD-form)?
Declarations of compliance – also called self -declarations – must be filled -in and signed by data
holders/service providers that have data sets or services listed in the data categories of the Delegated
Regulations and provide them on the National Access Point. The data holders/service providers declare
compliance with the Delegated Regulations by signing the self -declaration. For each data/service
provider affected by a Delegated Regulation one signed self -declaration is necessary per Delegated
Regulation.
The National Body/Competent Authority will use these self-declarations as input for their assessment
process (compliance assessment) to check if data holders and service providers comply with the
requirements of the Delegated Regulations.
[Link to templates]
1.6.2. Is a self-declaration required per organisation or per dataset?
Currently, a declaration per organisation shall be provided, supplemented by additional information
(appendix with data sets), and signed by an authorised signatory.
1.6.3. Are only authorised persons allowed to sign the self-declarations?
Yes, self -declarations have to be signed by authorised signatories who are determined by the
respective stakeholders.
1.6.4. Can third parties fulfil the declaration obligation?
Any entity providing data via the National Access Point can do so by proxy in accordance with
applicable agreements, for example through a third-party database or aggregator. This does not relieve
the original data holder of responsibilities regarding the quality of the original data being provided.

1.6.5. What is the “accompanying documentation”?
Accompanying documentation shall be provided by the data/service provider that provides (meta -)
data, in accordance with the respective Delegated Regulation, on the NAP. The information in the
accompanying documentation shall support the National Body/Comp etent Authority in carrying out
the compliance assessment. If existing organisational documents provide answers or information to
requested topics, they shall be added to the Annex of the accompanying documentation. Per
Delegated Regulation one template fo r the accompanying documentation was prepared within
NAPCORE.
[Link to templates]
1.7. What is a compliance assessment?
The National Bodies/Competent Authorities of the Member States carry out compliance assessments
to check if data and service providers are compliant with the requirements of the Delegated
Regulations (EU) 2017/1926, 2022/670, No 886/2013 and No 885/2013. I n some Member States a
financial penalty can be imposed to data/service providers by the National Body/Competent Authority
if the requirements of the Delegated Regulations are not met. The requirements of each Delegated
Regulation are listed in the compliance assessment forms set up in NAPCORE.
1.7.1. Who will be assessed by the National Bodies/Competent Authorities?
Providers of data and services must submit a dataset or a description of their (meta-) data to a National
Access Point (NAP) in accordance with the afore mentioned Delegated Regulations. The provision of
(meta-) data shall be accompanied by a ‘declaration of compliance’ and ‘accompanying
documentation’. The National Body or the Competent Authority assess if data/service providers, who
are selected by random sampling, meet the requirements of the Delegated Regulations.
When data is provided by a Proxy, it is still the original data provider, and thus not the proxy who will
be assessed.
1.7.2. What are Compliance Assessment (results) forms?
Compliance Assessment forms can be used by the National Body/Competent Authority as basis to carry
out compliance assessments. The compliance assessment forms include key questions for each article
of the Delegated Regulation, the theoretical and the content assessment as well as the requirements
to check the compliance. Compliance assessment results forms shall be used by the National
Body/Competent Authority during the compliance assessment process to indicate if the assessed
organisations comply with the articles of the Delegated Regulations.
Within NAPCORE, compliance assessment processes for the Delegated Regulations (EU) 2017/1926,
2022/670, No 886/2013 and No 885/2013 are still being harmonised. This will lead to a common
assessment procedure across Europe and allow for comparability of the assessment of international
private organisations. [Link to templates]

2. Delegated Regulations
2.1. MMTIS (2024/490 amending 2017/1926)
2.1.1. Services and modes of transport concerned
All transport modes are covered by this Delegated Regulation: public contracted and freely organised
transport services, as well as personal modes of transport, are covered. The annex to the MMTIS
(Multimodal Travel Information Services) Delegated Regulation lists the modes and means of transport
involved:
• Scheduled transport such as air transport, rail transport (including high -speed rail,
conventional rail and light rail), cable transport, long -distance coach transport, maritime
transport including ferries, inland waterway transport, metros, buses and trolleybuses.
• On-demand transport such as shuttle bus, shuttle ferry, on -demand ride services, cab, car -
sharing, car -pooling, car rental, shared rides, shared bikes, self -service bikes, bike rental,
electric scooters.
• Personal transport such as cars, motorcycles, bicycles, scooters and walking.
2.1.2. Does my organisation need to register data for MMTIS?
Yes, if you are a transport authority, a transport operator, a transport on demand service provider or
an infrastructure manager and have relevant data in digital format.
2.1.3. Does the MMTIS Delegated Regulation include the collection of data, i.e. the obligation
to digitise information?
No, the MMTIS Delegated Regulation only concerns the accessibility of existing digital machine -
readable data via the NAP, not the creation of new datasets which are not already in a digital machinereadable format. To be noted however that the revised ITS Directive (Annex III) mandates the creation
of certain static multimodal traffic data from 2026 onwards.
2.1.4. Which data must be provided regarding fares information parameters?
The Delegated Regulation 2017/1926 (MMTIS) aims to offer multi -modal travel information services
to users, also taking static fares into account. This means that static fare data that can be included in
a route planner to help calculate costs, if available, should be provided.
2.1.5. What is expected of cities and municipalities regarding MMTIS?
Cities and municipalities have the obligation to register their mobility related datasets according to the
Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/1926. This currently only applies to data that is already available in a
machine-readable format. It needs to be noted , however, that the revised ITS Directive (Annex III)
mandates the creation of certain static multimodal traffic data from 2026 onwards.
More specifically, this involves for example the following data categories:
• Points Of Interest (POI's) (related to transport information, e.g. airports, railway stations, ports
and other transport hubs)
• Cycle route network (segregated cycle paths, mixed traffic, mixed pedestrian and cycle path)
• Pedestrian and accessibility network

2.1.8. Parking operators
The required data:
(a) location search – for transport on demand and personal transport:
(i) location of parking places (on and off-street), including accessible parking places for persons

2.1.9. How are transport on demand service providers affected by the MMTIS Delegated
Regulation
Article 2 of the MMTIS DR provides a definition of “transport on demand service provider” and the
Annex includes a non-exhaustive list of examples of different kind of transport on demand. Whether a
transport on demand service provider is required to make a specific data type accessible via the NAP,
can be determined by examining the requirements specified in the different data types listed in the
Annex. This obligation exists only when the transport on demand service provider in its role as data
holder has the data available in a digital machine-readable format.
2.1.10. Car-sharing associations with a closed group of users (membership)
Are they affected by the Delegated Regulation (EU) 2017/1926? No, since the offer and services are
only available to selected users/members and cannot be used by the general public.

2.3. SRTI (886/2013)
2.3.1. Services and modes of transport concerned
The SRTI (Safety-Related Traffic Information) Delegated Regulation applies to:
• Public and/or private road operators: any legal entity or natural person participating in the
provision of road safety-related minimum universal traffic information;
• Public and/or private service providers: any legal entity or natural person participating in the
provision of road safety-related minimum universal traffic information services;
• Broadcasters: broadcasters dedicated to the provision of traffic information.
2.3.2. What should my organisation register on the NAP for SRTI?
Registration of the detected road safety related events or conditions on the NAP by the public and
private road operators and/or service providers is mandatory. The data should be provided in DATEX
II or any machine-readable format fully compatible and interoperable with DATEX II.

3. Data quality, format and requirements
3.1. What are the quality criteria being proposed?
The quality framework, which sets the criteria for data quality, developed by EU EIP is currently being
updated and should be accessible until the end of December 2024.
3.2. Data format requirements
You are required to provide the data in a machine -readable format. This means that information can
be read and processed directly, without any additional conversion step. Different data formats
required as per the Delegated Regulations (EU) are:
MMTIS (2017/1926):
• For dynamic travel and traffic data: SIRI and DATEX II
• For static, historic and observed travel and traffic data: NeTEx and DATEX II
RTTI (2022/670):
• Infrastructure: DATEX II, INSPIRE, TN-ITS,
• Networks: DATEX II
SRTI (886/2013): DATEX II, or any fully compatible and interoperable with DATEX II machine
readable format
SSTP (885/2013): DATEX II, or any fully compatible and interoperable with DATEX II machine
readable format.
Compatibility: If the data are provided in compatible format, information about how data can
be converted needs to be provided.
A general overview of the difference between the different standards/formats can be found in this link
https://transmodel-cen.eu/index.php/faq-general/
3.3. What should I do if my dataset is available in various formats?
If a dataset is available in more than one format it is better to use the version that is most compatible
with the Delegated Regulations (see question above chapter 3.3).

3.5. Which formats are considered machine-readable?
The formats must be machine-readable. The obligations of the Delegated Regulations require data to
be available in machine-readable formats. Formats are considered machine-readable when machines
can interpret the file format (e.g., CSV, and XML exports). T he formats specified in the Delegated
Regulations should be applied.
3.6. Can NeTEx be used for shared mobility? Are there examples or a manual for
this?
The NeTEx - part 5 format was specifically developed for the static data of "alternative modes" (=
demand-responsive modes such as shuttle bus, shuttle ferry, taxi, car -sharing, car-pooling, car-hire,
bike-sharing and bike-hire). This format allows the inclusion of information related to vehicles, fleets,
stations, access options, geofencing, booking information, rates, user accounts, etc.
Through this link you can find the detailed technical information about this format with also some
practical examples that can help with transforming data. It is also possible to watch the webinar.
Additional very important material for NeTEx -part 5 is the XML Schema Definition (XSD):
https://data4pt.org/w/index.php?title=NeTEX#NeTEx_Part_5_for_New_Modes. In NeTEx – CEN
Github, there are also some examples of XML files.
For real-time data, the SIRI format should be used.

The SRTI Delegated Regulation mentions explicitly that road safety -related minimum universal traffic

4.3.2. Licencing: Do I need to provide the data underlying my dataset/service on the NAP in
an open manner?
It is not mandatory to provide open access to the data underlying your dataset or service on the NAP.
Only the metadata and a link to your dataset or service need to be publicly visible on the NAP. The
data or service that you host on your own website does therefore not need to be publicly accessible.
You may ask for a contract or license for access to your data, whether or not in return for payment. If
this is the case, you need to indicate this information in the metadata fields for your dataset ‘Contract
or license’ and ‘License type’.
For SRTI (Safety-Related Traffic Information), however, it is mandatory that you make the category
'Minimum safety information' publicly available (on your own website) and free of charge . This is
stated in the European Delegated Act 886/2013 for SRTI:
‘Public road operators, service providers and broadcasters dedicated to traffic information shall provide
road safety-related minimum universal traffic information to end users prior to the provision of any
other non-safety-related traffic information. The information service shall be provided in such a way as
to ensure the widest reach of end users concerned by the given event or condition referred to in Article
3.’
4.4. Are there limits to the usage of data downloaded from the NAP?
If you are reusing data of the NAP, you have to follow a set of rules:
• You are obliged to mention the source and date of the last update of the dataset
• You are obliged to process the data in an impartial manner
Ensure that you do not unnecessarily limit the reusability of the data.
• Respect the user agreement.
• The terms and conditions of travel information services are defined in contractual agreements
between the travel information service providers. Financial compensations for the expenses
of linking travel information services should be reasonable and proportionate.
4.5. How can I contact a data holder?
If you have specific questions regarding a dataset, you can directly contact the data provider. The email
address of the data provider can be found in the metadata of that dataset. Please mention the dataset
your question relates to in your communication, as some of the data providers provide multiple
datasets on the NAP.

5. GDPR
5.1. How are data on the National Access Point provided in compliance with the
GDPR?
Data on the NAP do not contain personal information and comply with legal requirements. Data and
service providers only establish a link to the NAP; the dataset itself is not stored at the NAP. If the
personal data of a data/service provider are used or shown, this has to be accepted by this data/service
provider.
