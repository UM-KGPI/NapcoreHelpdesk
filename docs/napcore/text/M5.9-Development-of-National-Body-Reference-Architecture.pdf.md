Abstract
Milestone report M5.9 presents the status of a part of the work of Task 5.1 within WG 5 and elaborates
on the development and the general usage of the National Body reference Architecture. By the end of
2022, the first discussions about the strategy, creation process and related content of the task and the
next steps took place in order to archive a harmonised National Body/Competent Authority Reference
Architecture by the end of NAPCORE.
Due to the high number of open WG5 discussion points and harmonisation issues, as well as the future
relevant working topics of NAPCORE , creation of a final working tool represents a big challenge. To
address these challenges, multi-dimensional measures are required, e.g. technical, organisational,
communicational and strategic ones.
The Milestone M5.9 describes a ll process step s and achievement s of the National Body Reference
Architecture and indicates the ongoing developments and issue tracking process.

1. Introduction and Motivation
1.1. Background
In accordance with the requirements by the Delegated Regulations (DR) (EU) No 885/2013 (SSTP), No
886/2013 (SRTI), No 2015/962 and the updated version No 2022/670 (RTTI), No 2017/1926 (MMTIS)
supplementing the ITS Directive (2010/40/EU), Member States (MS) should designate a national body
or competent authority to assess whether the requirements set in the particular DR are fulfilled by the
respective designated stakeholders . Terms National Body and Competent Authority are used in the
delegated acts to refer to an entity performing similar tasks. For this reason, the term "National Body"
is used to refer to both "national body" and "competent authority" used in the delegated acts. The
main tasks of the National Bodies include receiving self -declarations, performing randomly selected
compliance assessments, and reporting to the MS.
In order to analyse the current European status of the compliance assessment of the National Body,
the NAPCORE WG5 organised a survey in 2022. The survey identified different existing compliance
assessment processes, used forms and accompanying documents as well as a variety of ways of
reporting to the Member States and the European Commission. Also, based on the analysis of national
legislations it was recogni sed that the setups of the National Bodies are different. Noteworthy, the
results of the survey showed that only Belgium, Germany and Norway ha ve started to establish
compliance assessment proced ures according to the ITS Directive and the corresponding D elegated
Regulations (DRs) . Given the variety of implemented national bodies throughout Europe, there are
significant challenges for harmonisation of their activities . However, as only three countries have
already started their activities of compliance assessment activities , it is quite easy to adopt the ir
harmonised processes in other existing National Bodies.
Bearing in mind the reported and analysed strong and weak points of the existing compliance
assessment practices among a few European countries , i t was recogni sed that a European -wide
compliance assessment framework needs to be developed. Understanding the needs for the
transparency and openness of the compliance assessment procedures and the communication of
information to the relevant stakeholders will improve the abilities of designated bodies for carrying
out the harmonised and high-quality compliance assessment.
The key activities of a National Body– receiving and processing a self -declaration and performing
random inspections – were described in an earlier Milestone report M5.3 - “Synchronised compliance
assessment processes and self -declaration forms“ of NAPCORE WG5. The earlier Milestone report
identified the main activities of a National Body and provided high-level descriptions of the workflow
for both activities. However, it did not aim to provide all viewpoints of a reference architecture,
FRAME-compliant descriptions or the level of detail of a complete reference architecture.
Descriptions of the process of receiving and processing a self -declaration provided in M5.3 were
utilised in the development of the functional, organisational and communication viewpoints of the
National Body Reference Architecture . The communicating entities and the information they ex -

• compliance assessment and random inspection processes
• methods and processes for reporting to Member States and the European Commission (EC).
1.2. Why is an architecture needed?
The National Body Reference Architecture is relevant for the harmonisation of the functionalities and
procedures of the designated bodies which are in charge of the compliance assessment. The
architecture is a concept and methodology based on t he European ITS Framework Architecture
(hereafter - FRAME Architecture).
The FRAME Architecture is a framework , which provides a systematic basis for planning ITS
implementations in Europe. It facilitates their integration when multiple systems are to be deployed
and helps to ensure interoperability across Europe. A distinctive feature of the FRAME Architecture is
that it is designed to have sub -sets created from it and is thus unlikely to be used in its entirety. It
contains more than one way of performing a service and the user can select the most appropriate se t
of functionalities to deliver it in that environment. Thus, the FRAME Architecture is a framework from
which specific models of integrated ITS implementations can be created in a systematic and common
manner. The FRAME Architecture does not mandate any physical or organisational structure in the
European Union. It comprises only a set of
- user needs, namely the set of requirements formulated in the ITS directive 2010/40/EU and
their DR (EU) No 885/2013 (SSTP), No 886/2013 (SRTI), No 2015/962 (RTTI) and No 2017/1926

(MMTIS) to the national body, competent to assess compliance with the requirements of the
particular DR on their territories.
- procedures or rules, showing how compliance assessment can be done.
The FRAME methodology is used to create an architecture. This methodology assists the creation of a
logically consistent functional view, and the creation of subsequent physical, communication and
organisational views based on the national body definitions. The FRAME Architecture comprises the
top-level requirements and functionality, for almost all the ITS applications and services to be
implemented in the EU. The FRAME Architecture could be used as a reference architecture for creating
more detailed architectures and designs that would be necessary for any particular ITS implementation
in the EU or at national level. The use of the FRAME Architecture increases the likelihood that newly
developed systems or components will be compatible and interoperable at European level.
1.3. Objectives
The main objective of this work was to create an architecture for the harmonisation of National Bodies
activities on the European level as well as increasing their effectiveness. From the perspective of the
National Body , the architecture defines its roles, functions, and processes . From a governance and
compliance assessment point of view, it defines the minimum requirements to be met at the national
level in aim to achieve the harmonisation of National Bodies activities as well as their interoperability
at European level. An architecture should be seen as a functional framework providing mechanisms
that can be customised by the national bodies according to their individual requirements. The National
Body Reference Architecture aims to support the National Bodies
- by providing guidance to set up national body by taking into account the requirements at
national level and requirements for harmonisation at European level
- by establishing trustworthy relationships between Data/Service providers, NAP operators
and national bodies
- by facilitating the engagement with Data/Service providers for submitting self-declarations to
the NAP
- by establishing a governance structure that does not require a centralised approach.
The main principles for designing a National Body Reference Architecture were:
- simplicity – the main objects and relations between them corresponds to national body,
responsible for carrying out the compliance assessment procedures, business model
- transferability – replication of the architecture by adopting specific requirements/rules at
national level
- understandability – no required experience or special knowledge needed in the design of the
National Body Reference Architecture.
By using National Body Reference Architecture the following benefits for the National Bodies are
expected:

1.4. Tooling
The FRAME Architecture consists of three elements: architecture, methodology , and tools. It is a
repository of user needs and functions, which are grouped into several functional areas. The FRAME
Methodology employs the FRAME Architecture while utilising the building blocks, glossary and
definitions to provide a clear procedure for the ITS archi tect to develop ITS service architectures. The
FRAME Tool enables the ITS architect to create ITS architectures as sub-sets of the FRAME Architecture
in order to implement one or more ITS services.
The FRAME Architecture Tool v.5.1.1 based on Enterprise Architect file (*.eapx) shall be used for the
creation of the ITS Architecture. This tool
- is flexible in combining different architecture views and objects
- allows graphical modelling
- uses UML basic modelling language
- is able to produce standardised output documents, i.e. XML
- is widely used by the ITS community.
The FRAME Architecture tool contains the following packages: FRAME Metamodel, FRAME Repository
and Reference Architecture models which already created (e.g., eCall Reference Architecture, C -ITS
Reference Architecture, etc.) or to be developed in future (e.g., NAP Reference Architecture, National
Body Reference Architecture, etc.). In aim to offer a common compliance assessment service , the
different stakeholders/actors involved in compliance assessment process must cooperate at a business
level as well as on a technical level and their architectures should be interoperable at all levels of
cooperation.

2. Methodology
2.1. General working procedure
The process began by collecting and selecting the material relevant for the development of the
reference architecture. Much of this work had already been performed in earlier tasks of NAPCORE
WG5 which had provided e.g. an analysis of the DRs and descriptions of the main processes of the
National Body. At the start of the work, there was a brief review of the available architecture tools and
paradigms. After the review, the FRAME architecture methodology was selected for developing the
reference architecture.
The organisational, functional and communication views of the national reference architecture were
developed by a team assigned for the task in a series of work meetings. The work meetings were led
by a facilitator using the Enterprise Architecture softwar e who created the descriptions of various
architecture elements based on an open and interactive discussion with other team members
participating in the work meeting. After the different viewpoints, architecture elements and their
connections had been comp leted in a series of work meetings, the early draft version of the
architecture was edited to produce the first complete version of the work. This stage included e.g.
addressing inconsistencies in the architecture descriptions, adding missing information to the
architecture elements, adding missing connections between the different elements of the architecture
and exporting a description of the architecture from the Enterprise Architect software as a pdf file.
The first version of the architecture was then sent for comments to the members of NAPCORE WG5.
In addition to a description of the architecture (as a word file), WG5 members were provided with
instructions for providing their feedback.
2.2. Selecting sources
The sources of information used in the architecture development work included Milestone reports
created in NAPCORE WG5. Earlier NAPCORE WG5 documents provided descriptions of the main
processes of the National Body (M5.3), draft versions of the documents to be used for submitting and
evaluating a self-declaration (M5.3), good practices of already operational National Bodies and related
recommendations for harmonised compliance assessment (M5.2). The project team also had access
to not-yet-published results from NAPCORE WG5 work such as the classification of National Body
maturity levels developed in NAPCORE Task 5.1.
In addition to the NAPCORE documents, several other relevant sources were identified and used in the
development of the reference architecture. Latest versions of the DRs (EU) No 885/2013 (SSTP), No
886/2013 (SRTI), No 2015/962 (RTTI) and No 2017/1926 (MMTIS) were used as a reference to
understand the requirements for the National Body. National regulations affecting the operation and
establishment of the national body (e.g. an English translation of the Administrative Procedure Act in

regulation de scribed the stakeholders and their national tasks on a high level but did not provide a
detailed description of the National Body or its operation.

covered by the reference architecture. In the beginning of the task, a decision was made to focus the
reference architecture on the organisation al, functional , and communication views included in the
FRAME architecture model. When starting the analysis, technology neutrality and transferability
between Member States were identified as relevant requirements for the reference architecture.

The work to create the architecture was started by developing the organisational view first. The entities
involved were identified based on the DRs and the main processes of a National Body (receiving and
processing a self -declaration and random inspections ) described in Milestone report M5.3 -
Synchronised compliance assessment processes and self -declaration forms , based on the principle
shown in Figure 1Figure 1: National Body Reference Architecture developing principle. After the
organisational viewpoint had been described, the work was continued by creating descriptions of the
functional and communications viewpoints.
The Physical View was omitted in the reference architecture because it was expected to be difficult or
impossible to identify a set of physical elements and connections between them that would meet the
national requirements of all Member States. Additionally, these requirements might conflict with each
other. The content of Milestone report M5.2 and earlier discussions inside W G5 indicated that
different Member States have either organised the National Body in different ways or are likely to do
so. For example, a Member State may have only one organisation performing the tasks of the National
Body or different organisations responsible for different DRs. There are also differences between the
Member States regarding the distribution of tasks between the National Body and the NAP. In some
Member States, the NAP , as Competent Authority , provides information and support for data and
service providers in preparing and submitting a self-declaration while in other Member States, this is a
task of the National Body.
An intentional decision was made to not include the Security View in the reference architecture. The
security requirements for the processing and receiving of self -declarations and performing random
inspections are partly common in all EU countries, e.g. referring to the integrity of the data, and partly
Member State specific, for instance regarding the confidentiality of self-declarations and the results of
the random inspections. In some Member States, documents of public authorities are publicly available
upon request, unless there is a lawful reason to keep the document confidential (e.g. in Finland, Act
on the Openness of Government Activities) while other Member States do not have such a
requirement. The implementation of security mechanisms is also dependent on the physical entities
involved and the systems the Member States use for receiving, processing and archiving self -
declarations.
When developing the reference architecture, the activities of the National Body were foreseen to
contribute to the safety of road users by improving compliance of services and data to the quality
requirements defined in the DRs. While the activities of the National Body were foreseen to make an
indirect contribution to safety of road users, no direct safety-related requirements were identified. For
this reason, the safety perspective was not included in the first version of the national body reference
architecture.

3. The Architecture
In the beginning of the process of service design, there is often a rough idea or an obligation to create
some service with a certain functionality. A lot of business -related aspects are not clear yet, but of
crucial importance to the success of the National Body tasks. Taking this into account, the Motivational
FRAME provides a flexible guide to structure the ideas and identify necessary parts for the service
creation and delivery. The following four basic principles apply to the process of creating the Business
FRAME:
1. The order of filling in the building blocks is rather a recommendation than a strict rule.
2. The elements within the building blocks are not mandatory. They should be used as deemed
useful, although it is recommended to also note the decision to leave an aspect out.
3. Often only an unclear idea of a service exists, so that the building blocks cannot be filled in
directly but can be completed at a later stage, when more information is available.
4. The work on the business aspects is always an iterative task. Often, the definition of one part
of the Business FRAME has implications on another, which might not be specified yet.

3.1. The Motivational Layer
The motivational layer shows the initial position of the system development, the involved stakeholders
as well as the expected added values and desired business goals. These inputs result in the service
description of the National Body or Competent Authority (NB/CA). However, National B odies are
established by the government or recognised by authorities to oversee, manage, regulate, or represent
various aspects of national affairs. Therefore, the motivational layer should represent the motivational
aspects of the ITS directive 2010/40/EU related roles in a general way.
The motivational layer enables the user to see the overarching picture of a system at once including
all relevant stakeholders, main goals and the general context.

Beside general description of roles, intentions and expected results can be clearly described in the

• The ITS Stakeholder: describes stakeholders affected by the ITS System
• The Business Expectation: key results which are expected by the stakeholders
• The Actor: a connector element to further FRAME views

3.3. The Functional View
The functional view is a key part of any architecture. It shows the most important functionalities of a
system, which are essential to operate, maintain and host an ITS system. In addition to the broadly
defined functionalities , the data flows, which involve the trans fer of information in a specific order,
are shown and define d. Each functionality needs at least one IN and OUT data flow. The starting and
the end point of a functionality view are called “Terminator”, as they terminate a dataflow. If a
functionality needs a storage of elements, they are also connected via data flows to the right
functionality. A storage can be defined as any element which can store information, data, documents,
or dynamic information.

3.4. The Communication View
The communication view represents a very important part of a system architecture. It describes
defined processes and the communication steps between allocated roles. The general method to
depict it is to create a flow diagram per defined process. A process flow chart needs to be allocated
to a functionality within the Functional View.
The communication view enables the user to understand how a process is defined and which roles
are involved in a specific part of a process.

4. Future support and issues tracking
Since a goal of the NAPCORE project is the harmoni sation of the methodologies for carrying out
National Body activities in the Member States, there is a need for reference architecture. Describing
the architecture provides an opportunity to harmonise the management of datasets received by data
providers as well as the compliance assessment processes. The development work on the National
Body Reference Architecture began in 2023, the current version is a draft.
Today's version has been prepared by the members of the Working Group, and the basis for its creation
have been the materials prepared by other task and working groups, the ITS directive and feedback
from the members of the WG. The Milestone report addresses a wide range of readers, understanding
the architecture, however, is facilitated with a background of technical knowledge.
The work on the National Body Reference Architecture will continue within WG5 until the end of the
NAPCORE project. This involved collecting feedback and, if necessary, making changes to the current
architecture draft. Changes in EU legislation may also be taken into account and result in the adaption
of the architecture:
4.1. Results from the National Body Reference Architecture work
Milestone Report 5.9 contains the work procedure, definitions and status of the work carried out so
far. WG5 has drafted an action plan to propose how the architecture will be updated once the project
is finished. The plan is to adopt the CC-BY-4.0 license model.
The Enterprise Architect File contains: National Body Reference Architecture based on the FRAME

XML exports directly from Enterprise Architect can be used for transferring the National Body
Reference Architecture in other architecture designing tools. XML is a free format and open for use by

The architecture Word export exported directly from Enterprise Architect contains all architecture

reference architecture has been published on the NAPCORE project website and on GITHUB, National
Bodies will be able to adopt it on a uniform basis. With the project participation of EU partner
countries, the architecture is likely to be adopted more widely. Moreover, the WG members anticipate

5. Conclusion and next steps at this stage
The National Body Reference Architecture is a kind of technical, organisational and functional guideline
to create and harmoni se national body activities according to the DRs in the different organisational
structures.
The Milestone 5.9 - “Development of National Body Reference Architecture” shows the analysis an d
development towards a reference architecture which brings all NAPCORE WG5 National Body
harmonisation activities together. The architecture contains all harmonised and necessary information
which were available from NAPCORE and especially the WG5.
By using a well -known software, Enterprise Architect, and a best fitting toolkit, FRAME, the
requirements of an understandable and future proof architecture are ensured.
The National Body Reference Architecture can be used for three different use cases:
• First, to create a harmonised National Body from scratch by implementing the d escription of
any functional and organisational elements which are needed for an operational harmoni sed
NB from the architecture.
• Second, to h armonise an existing NBs by adopting already existing NB functionalities and
processes according to the developed National Body Reference Architecture.
• Third, establish basis for comparison of NBs by splitting complex functionalities and processes
up. These separated parts can be compared between NBs.
Due to the ongoing developments in work regarding the harmonisation of the NBs, this document
describes the first version of the architecture. The status of the National Body Reference Architecture
is ongoin g and the continuous new inputs, e.g. the adoption to the new release ITS directive, from
WG5 to the architecture will result in updates of the National Body Reference Architecture in 2024. A
feedback loop was generated for the clarification of open questions. In parallel to this development, it
is recommended to conduct empirical research of usability on a voluntary basis to gather specific
feedback from NB operators. Results such as common view, best understanding and experience will
be used for further developments of the National Body Reference Architecture.
