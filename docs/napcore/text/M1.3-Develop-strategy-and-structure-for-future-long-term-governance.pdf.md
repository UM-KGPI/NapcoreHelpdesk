Abstract
The aim of Task 1.3 “Future and sustainable governance structure of the NAP/NB platform” is to
explore options for a sustainable long-term governance structure for NAPCORE activities. In 2023, first
steps were taken towards establishing this structure. The groundwork included identifying activities
for a subsequent NAPCORE project and a long -term organisational structure. Extensive research and
analysis were conducted on various potential governance structures, whether connected, integrated,
or independent from the European Institutions . In addition , a set of questions was developed and
analysed to define the purpose of such a structure. Furthermore, ideas and insights were discussed
with key stakeholders, such as the NAPCORE Steering Committee, the NAPCORE Advisory Board and
the European Commission . These discussions generally revealed support for the long-term
continuation of NAPCORE activities.
The recommendations arising from this task focus on a NAPCORE follow-up project as well as options
for a long-term governance structure. Regarding a continuity of NAPCORE activities , it is planned to
prolong the current project phase for another year. Moreover, a proposal for a follow -up project
(2026-2028) will be submitted (the respective call is expected to open in Q3 or Q4 of 2024). Referring
to the long -term governance structure, intense discussions on the purpose and objectives with
NAPCORE partners, Member States, the Advisory Board the European Commission still need to take
place in 2024. The goal is to identify the best fit for such a structure and develop an implementation
plan.

1. Introduction
The aim of Task 1.3 “Future and sustainable governance structure of the NAP/NB platform” is to
explore options for a sustainable long-term governance structure of the NAP (National Access point) /
NB (National Body) platform. More specifically, a strategic and operational implementation plan for a
harmonised European-wide operation of the NAP/NB platform, which will form the base of the overall
governance structure, s hall be set up, considering future management, financing, membership
opportunities and links to external actors. In order to achieve these objectives, the national needs and
requirements regarding this long -term governance structure, national NAP/NB strategies as well as
new requirements and experiences, which arose during the NAPCORE project, were taken into
consideration. Moreover, the work carried out within the Task is based on the results of Tasks 1.1 and
1.2, Working Group (WG) 3 , and strongly aligned with Activity H.1 (Steering Committee Support).
Concrete actions, which will be described in detail in Chapter 2 “Methodology and process
description”, included the identification of activities for a NAPCORE follow -up project, the
identification of activities for the long -term governance structure and the research of possible
governance structures.
1.1. Vision of NAPCORE
It has become apparent that the existing NAPs are quite different in their setup and data access
interfaces. Also, the data formats and standards used differ throughout Europe. To work on a better
alignment, the National Access Point Coordination Organisation for Europe (NAPCORE) project was
started. NAPCORE is co-financed by a Programme Support Action under the European Commission’s
Connecting Europe Facility. NAPCORE has been launched as coordination mechanism to improve
interoperability of the NAPs as backbone of European mobility data exchange. The project improves
the interoperability of mobility data in Europe with mobility data standard harmonisation and
alignment. Also, NAPCORE increases access and expands availability to mobility related data by
coordinated data access and better harmonisation of the E uropean NAPs. Furthermore, NAPCORE
empowers N APs and N Bs by defining and implementing common procedures and strategy,
strengthening the position and the role of NAPs, supporting steps towards the creation of European -
wide solutions to better facilitate the use of EU-wide data.
NAPCORE has been created in the spirit of consultation and cooperation. It includes 73 participants:
33 Beneficiaries covering 2 6 EU Member States (MS), 3 associated partners and 37 Implementing
Bodies. The initial runtime of the Programme Support Action is until the end of 2024, but the goal is to
establish a long-lasting and future-oriented platform organisation.
The vision of NAPCORE is, to remain active to maintain and increase interoperability amongst the NAPs
and NBs, as long as the countries/MS are responsible for operating NAPs. By that, the NAPs will become
a solid part of the digital infrastructure in Europe and can also play a major role in a futur e European
Mobility Data Space.

2. Methodology and process description
In order to achieve the objectives of Task 1.3 as described in Chapter 1 of this report, a detailed
Working Programme (WP) was set up. For the year 2023, the following activities were foreseen in the
WP: a) the identification of NAPCORE follow-up activities for a project structure, b) the identification
of NAPCORE activities for a long -term structure, and c) the identification of possible governance
structures. In the following paragraphs, these activities will be described in more detail.
2.1. Questionnaires to identify activities for a follow -up project and a long -term
governance structure
In order to identify activities for a NAPCORE follow -up project as well a long -term governance
structure, two questionnaires were developed. The first one was sent to all WG and Activity leads and
NAPCORE Ambassadors and aims to identify key elements for a future continuation of NAPCORE (see
Annex 1: Questionnaire template for Working Group Leaders/Activity Leaders/Ambassadors to identify
activities for a follow-up project and a long-term governance structure). Thereby, a distinction is made
between tasks and activities that were not part of the first phase of NAPCORE, requiring a subsequent
NAPCORE follow-up project, and those that will need attention in the ongoing, long-term coordination
among the NAPs/NBs. Specifically, the WG/Activity leads and Ambassadors were asked to come up
with activity names, a brief description of the activity as well as requirements and preconditions crucial
to achieve the activity. Generally, the activities proposed for a NAPCORE follow-up project ought to be
of “project-style-character", i.e., have a defined scope and end, be result-oriented and have an agreed
budget and timeframe, while the activities for long -term coordination could be characterised as
continuous, never-ending activities, which are needed to maintain a certain level of interoperability.

3. Findings
In this chapter, the findings emerging out of the questionnaires and research on possible governance
structures will be illustrated.
3.1. Activities for a NAPCORE follow-up project
In this section , first insights regarding a NAPCORE follow -up project will be presented. Hereby, it is
important to note that the current NAPCORE period is not yet completed and hence, the
recommendations emerging out of the project are either not fully developed or are still considered
preliminary. Two main ways of continuation became obvious: First, gaps and further topics for
harmonisation need to be further addressed in a continuation project, as in NAPCORE’s first period not
all emerging topics could be dealt with. In addition, the work of NAPCORE’s first phase, revealed gaps
which require the continuation in a follow -up project. Second, the need for implementation support
became obvious. Only focusing on the development of recommendations is not covering the need of
countries to get support (help as well as financial resources) for im plementing the recommendations
targeted to achieve a certain level of interoperability.
First, for the follow -up project, the importance of an interrelation between the NAPs and ot her
frameworks on the European level, e.g. , the European Mobility Data Space, European Access Points
and the Alternative Fuels Infrastructure Regulation – all not being scope of the ITS directive – became
evident. Moreover, the pilot testing of principles developed and achieved during NAPCORE, e.g., new
quality frameworks or data pricing guidelines, should be a main component of a NAPCORE follow -up
project. Regarding the topic of Compliance Assessment, activities for a follow-up project should focus
on simplification mechanisms/processes as well as enforcement strategies and actions in case of noncompliance. Concerning data exchange formats, future attention should lie on training and education
activities on the regional and local level and a stronger integration of (among others) the rail sector.
Furthermore, it will be crucial to remain a certain degree of openness to newly arising spotlight topics,
new data categories and potential new ambassador activities.
Second, as is has become apparent that providing implementation support for the MS regarding the
recommendations coming out of NAPCORE will be central in the future , special attention towards
implementation is needed. More precisely, support in the implementation of the NAP/NB reference
architecture, the DCAT -AP metadata catalogue, the automated metadata extraction, data gap
mitigation techniques, the e lectronic submission of self -declarations and semi -automated pre -
validation tools will be needed.

3.2. Activities for a long-term governance structure
The activities identified for a long-term governance structure are characterised by supporting the MS
in maintaining or reaching the level of interoperability and harmonisation a chieved and agreed on
during NAPCORE. Specifically, the governance structure should serve to maintain the level of NAP
interoperability, maintain data exchange standards, coordinate multi -national Compliance
Assessments, host and maintain the tools provided on the NAPCORE website (e.g., UVAR box) and work
out templates for Service -Level-Agreements with service providers in order to establish a long -term
cooperation. Moreover, the governance structure should aim to monitor newest evolvements in
technology and policy, e.g. , the revision of Delegated Regulations and continuously update and
enhance the NAPCORE recommendations accordingly. Additionally, links to external stakeholders as
well as basic governance processes, including decision-making processes and secretariat activities,
including communication, should be maintained within the long-term structure.
Also, the recent revision of the ITS Directive (EU) 2023/2661 and specifically the new Artic le 5 (3)
explicitly calls for Member States to
“... cooperate, for instance through Union supported coordination projects, and, where
necessary, with relevant stakeholders, on operational aspects of the implementation of the
specifications adopted by the Commission, such as standards and Union harmonis ed profiles,
common definitions, common metadata, common quality requirements and aspects related to
the interoperability of NAPs’ architectures, common data exchange conditions, secure access
and common training and outreach activities. In respect of requ irements for data providers,
data users and ITS service providers set out in the specifications, Member States shall also
cooperate on practices for assessing compliance with those requirements, on developing
mechanisms for compliance enforcement and on issues concerning cross-border cooperation."
With 15 out of 18 submitted questionnaires from the MS indicat ing a “very high” or “high” level of
consent for setting up a stable organisation for NAPCORE, the overall level of consent for establishing
a NAPCORE long -term governance structure it can be considered high (see Figure 1). Taking into
account the answers submitted by the SCOM members, it can be stated that the organisational
structure should diverge from a project-based arrangement, and the continuation of NAPCORE “as it
is” is not favoured or recommended. Furthermore, SCOM members strongly recommend that
NAPCORE learns from past experiences and identifies the most effective solution, depending on the
task, to sustain interoperability levels. That does not necessarily mean setting up a new organisational
structure; there might also be opportunities to leverage existing structures. Moreover, also the degree
of willingness of the MS to contribute to such a governance structure can be generally described as
high, although hesitation was stated due to the uncertainty of required resources (see Figure 2).

3.4. Description of various governance structures and analysis of their potential
to serve as possible structure for the long -term governance of NAP - / NB-relatedactivities
After three years of operation, the NAPCORE project has demonstrated its ability to unite the 27
Members States in a dynamic , effective, and practical collaboration, yielding convincing and ongoing
results. From both a strategic and technical point of view, NAPCORE has made its mark on the European
mobility landscape by drawing on the expertise of the representatives of the participating Member
States. A consensus seems to exist among the stakeholders (among others NAPCORE partners, EC,
members of the AB etc.) on the need to perpetuate NAPCORE beyond the project phase and to create
a sustainable long-term governance structure that can continue to take on the role of a coordination
platform and be the preferred partner of the sector for the development of strategies and technologies
related to the development of NAPs.
In this context, Task group 1.3 looked at various governance structures that could be envisaged to
sustain central NAPCORE activities on the long run. A first step entailed identifying elements to assess
and compare the different structures. The participants identified the following elements:
- Type of structure : definition of the structure, of the framework and of the main high -level
objectives
- Goals: description of the goals in a more specific way
- Relation with the EC: is the structure independent from, linked or integrated to the European
institutions
- Right of initiatives: does the structure have a right of initiative or is its working program defined
(limited) by a higher authority
- Possible members of the organi sation: type of possible members (MS, public /private
organisation, cities, etc.)
- Territorial scope
- Legal personality
- Liability (in case of debts)
- (Possible) Funding/Financial resources
- Financial obligations: what are they, can the structure generate revenues
- Staffing: what are the needs and obligations regarding staff
- Headquarter obligations (legal)
- Right to take sub-contractors (public procurement)
- If right to take sub-contractor, where this entity will be allowed to operate (only in the country
where the structure is registered or not)
- Governance rules/Structure: obligations related to meetings, decision process, structure, etc.
- Necessary steps for the creation of the structure (and timeline)
- Constitutional document ( e.g., grant agreement, cooperation agreement, articles of the
associations, etc.)
- Tax aspects
- Jurisdiction responsible in case of law issue/conflicts resolution: mediation, arbitrage, etc.

European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC)
European Digital Infrastructure Consortium5, or EDIC, is a brand-new instrument made available to MS
under the Digital Decade Policy Programme 2030 to speed up and simplify the setup and
implementation of multi-country projects6, aiming at facilitating the achievement of the Digital Decade
general objectives and targets. As this is a new mechanism, such a consortium does not exist yet. The
information gathered is therefore theoretical, given for information only, and may evolve as the EDIC

The EDIC’s framework supports the implementation of the European Data Spaces. An EDIC may
implement a multi-country project by deploying joint infrastructure, delivering services and bringing
together – as considered appropriate by the founding Member States – public entities, private entities,
final users and industry. Each EDIC will be established by a decision of the EC upon the application of
at least three MS and Commission approval. It will receive a legal personality.
Regarding membership, only MS that contribute financially or non -financially shall be eligible to
become members of the EDIC. Such MS shall have voting rights. Following the adoption of a decision
setting up an EDIC, other MS may become members at any time, on fair and reasonable terms to be
specified in the statutes of the EDIC. MS that provide neither a financial nor a non -financial
contribution may join an EDIC as observers by notifying the EDIC. Such MS shall not have voting rights.
Membership of an EDIC may be open to entities other than MS, which may include third countries,
international organisations of European interest, and public or private entities, as specified in the
statutes of the EDIC. Where entities other than MS are members of an EDIC, MS shall hold jointly the
majority of the voting rights in the assembly of members, regardless of the amount of contributions
from entities other than MS.
Regarding governance7, several mandatory elements should be respected. MS should jointly hold the
majority of voting rights. Statutory seat will be located in a MS that is a member providing financial or
non-financial contribution. The EDIC will have an Assembly of Members, which has full decision-making
powers, adopts annual budget, work plan and implementing rules. It will have a Director (executive
body and legal representative).
An EDIC shall have at least the following two bodies:
- An assembly of members made up of the MS, other entities, and of the EC, the assembly being
the body having full decision-making powers, including the adoption of the budget;
- A director, appointed by the assembly of members, as the executive body and le gal
representative of the EDIC.
The EC shall participate in the deliberations of the assembly of members, without itself having voting
rights. However, where a centrally managed Union programme contributes financially to a multi -
country project, the EC shall have a right of veto against decisions of the assembly related only to
actions financed under centrally managed Union programmes.

planning.
The EGTCs were installed by the Regulation (EC) 1082/2006 of the European Parliament and of the

EGTC is to facilitate and promote territorial cooperation, in particular between its members, including
one or more of the cross-border, transnational and interregional strands of cooperation, with the aim
of strengthening economic, social and territorial cohesion in the EU.
Generally speaking, the tasks of an EGTC are aligned with the objectives outlined above. Tasks may
include specific acts of territorial cooperation between its members, with or without financial support
from the Union. An EGTC may be tasked with implementing programmes co-financed by the European
Union, through the European Regional Development Fund, the European Social Fund and/or the
Cohesion Fund, or other cross -border cooperation projects that may or may not have EU funding.
Examples of such activities include running cross-border transport facilities or hospitals, implementing
or managing cross-border development projects, and sharing expertise and good practices.

- MS or authorities at national level;
- Regional authorities;
- Local authorities;
- Public undertakings or bodies governed by public law;
- Undertakings entrusted with operations of services of general economic interest;
- National, regional or local authorities, or bodies or undertakings from third countries (subject
to specific conditions);
- Associations consisting of bodies belonging to one or more of these categories.

members. EGTCs act on behalf of their members, who adopt their statutes by means of special
conventions outlining the organisation and activities of the EGTC. As a minimum requirement, an EGTC

director, who represents the EGTC and acts on its behalf. Furthermore, the powers of EGTCs are limited

regulatory powers, cannot be transferred to an EGTC. The assembly adopts the EGTC’s annual budget

experts. Members are financially liable for any debts, in proportion to their contribution to the budget.

AISBL
With the abundance of European bodies in Brussels, the Capital of Europe is the second largest city in
terms of housing of international associations. The category encompasses various entities such as
professional associations, foundations, and NGOs. This specificity prompted the Belgian authorities to
create a special legal form to respond to the reality on the ground and the demands of a sector. An
AISBL ( “Association International sans but lucratif ” in French) is a Belgian legal structure for
international non-profit association.
An international non-profit association is a group of natural or legal persons, Belgians and foreigners,
pursuing a disinterested goal of international utility (non-profit international goal). It must have at least
two members and has legal personality under Belgian (and therefore EU) law. It can enjoy all general
public and private rights of legal persons. The association has a registered office in Belgiu m. All the
incorporation documents must be written in German, French and/or Dutch, depending on the region
in which the corporate seat is located. The principle of freedom of contract will apply.
The purpose and the activities it proposes to carry out must be mentioned in the “Articles of
Association”. As soon as a common project is defined, the Articles of Association, which represent the
basic principles of the organisation, must be drawn up in a notarial deed. The articles will be signed by
the founding members and the notary. The AISBL only obtains a legal personality when it is recognised
by a royal decree. The process of obtaining the King’s signature implies that the creation process can
be long. The intervention of a notary and legal steps for approval represent costs (approximately
between 2000 and 2500 euros). No capital is needed.

4.1. Recommendations for a possible NAPCORE follow-up project
Based on the results of the questionnaires, describe d in Chapter 3.1 and 0, the subsequent
recommendations for continuation of NAPCORE activities have been formulated.
1.
Considerations should be made to request a one-year prolongment of NAPCORE in its current phase
as a coordination project. Several reasons lead to this plan.
1) Late start: The project has had a late s tart, by having a signed Grant Agreement only in the end of
2021, even though the original starting date was in April 2021. Due to the size of the project , and
having 73 partners including Implementing Bodies and Associated Partners, getting the Grant
Agreement signed was a rather complex task.
2) COVID-19 restrictions: The project start fell in a period with severe restrictions due to COVID -19.
Without the possibility to meet in person, it took more time to get the work distributed and get started
in full speed.
3) High number of partners to coordinate: Some processes which require agreements amongst all MS
took and continue to take more time than expected.
4) Full working more achieved in 2023 only: NAPCORE reaches its full speed and visibility only in 2023.
It would be unfortunate to lose this momentum and may not send a positive signal to NAPCORE
partners, the EC, and external stakeholders if the project concludes in 2024, only to restart in 2026.
2.
The EC is envisaging a continuation of the NAPCORE activities for the period of 2026 -2028 and is
planning to launch a call for this in mid to end 2024. The call and subsequent project should
incorporate the recommendations presented by NAPCORE. Recommendations include building upon
NAPCORE achievements while strengthening the focus on multi -modal topics , including rail . A
reconsideration of the current structure of NAPCORE is necessary, also to be well positioned for dealing
with additional requirements. To be prepared for answering the call for a continuation of NAPCORE
and continue the work on the long-term governance structure, a task force will be set up that will work
out a proper concept for SCOM decision by June 2024.
3.
The MS are encouraged and supported to participate in implementation projects (e.g. , corridor
projects, national projects) in order to implement NAPCORE recommendations . This has to happen
in parallel, as the implementation of NAPCORE recommendations on a national level will very likely
not be part of a NAPCORE follow-up project.
4.2. Recommendations for a long-term governance structure
On several occasions, partners ask why it was necessary to consider the choice of a long-term structure
for NAPCORE at this stage of the project. The answer is simple. Many strategic discussions are currently
taking place in the field of mobility at the European level : A European Road Agency, an EDIC for
Mobility and Logistics Data, the European Mobility Data Space, European Access Point s. These are
some of the many topics under discussions and negotiations right now, at a time when NAPCORE is
also examining its future. In addition, the revised ITS Directive (EU) 2023/2661 and specifically the new

Article 5 (3) includes a very specific proposal for a continuous cooperation mechanism amongst the

Hence, it is not a question if NAPCORE will be continued in a long -term manner, but rather how this
continuation will be executed. Subsequently, the purpose and objectives of the long-term continuation
will lead to its concrete form a nd structure. At this stage, several types of structure appear to be a
possible solution for a long-term governance structure for NAPCORE. This is good news, as it opens up
opportunities for partners to opt for the solution that they feel best meets the existing and future
needs and expectations regarding NAPCORE, but also t o choose the solution that best matches their
capacity for commitment.
In order to define the purpose of a NAPCORE long-term governance structure, a set of questions, which
will be briefly reflected on in this section , was collected. These reflections do not provide definite
answers to the questions raised, but are rather to be seen as a starting point to get a clearer
understanding of the objectives of a long-term governance structure.
• Will the future of NAPCORE consist only of technical coordination or also strategic positioning?
• What could an overarching, interlinking NAPCORE layer (“umbrella”) look like? There are
several options of what an overarching NAPCORE layer could be composed of. The options
vary from a rather strong organisational structure like an organisation to a rather loose
structure such as a multi-party agreement.
• Should the long -term governance of NAPCORE only involve MS/countries or also additional
stakeholders?
• Will NAPCORE move towards centralised implementations in the future? These
implementations shall go beyond current activities such as the NAPCORE Mobility Data Days
and operating tools, like the interoperability demonstrators, and could for instance include a
metadata portal or a common body for multi-national compliance assessment.
• How should NAPCORE be positioned in the stakeholder environment? A NAPCORE long-term
governance structure could take on various roles in the European stakeholder environment in
the mobility sector. It could either maintain strong links with external stakeholders, such as
private ITS service providers, and represent a strong negotiation partner for them, take on the
task of facilitating discussion between different stakeholders or not have a say in discussions
with external stakeholders at all and be more neutral/agnostic.
• What ambitions does NAPCORE have for being more active outside of Europe? Currently,
NAPCORE is involved in discussions with the World Bank and non -European countries due to
their expressed interest in NAPCORE's activities. This raises the question whether NAPCORE’s
focus should remain purely on the European level or also be oriented towards other parts of
the world.
The purpose of this report is to explore options for a sustainable long -term governance structure for
NAPCORE activities. It gives an initial overview of NAPCORE partners' expectations regarding the future
of NAPCORE. It also provides food for thought rega rding the possible tasks, roles and potential
governance structures to perpetuate and improve the current coordination between the 27 MS in
areas relating to the implementation of the ITS Directive and its Delegated Regulations. Here below,
next steps are proposed to continue the ongoing process of reflection and refine the position of the
partners regarding the future of NAPCORE. On this basis, the objective shall be to reach a consensus,
to define the necessary development steps/phases and to initiate it with the relevant stakeholders
within the relevant timeframe.

5. Conclusion and next steps
Regarding NAPCORE continuation, the initial step involv es planning a one -year prolongation of the
current project phase, motivated by several reasons detailed in Chapter 0. In addition, a follow-up to
NAPCORE is envisaged within another CEF project in the period of 2026 -2028. Within this phase, a
reconsideration of the current project structure as well as the inclusion of new topics will be necessary.
As for the long-term governance structure of NAPCORE, this report highlights its significance. The main
goal for 2024 is to get a clear picture of the purpose and the objectives of this structure. The questions
raised in Chapter 4.2 will be examined in detail. Moreover, intensive consultation of the NAPCORE
partners, the MS, the AB , and the EC will take place. Subsequently, the best fit for a long -term
organisational structure for NAPCORE activities will be identified. The objective and characteristics of
the structure as well as an implementation plan will be presented in Milestone Report M1.4 by the end
of 2024.
