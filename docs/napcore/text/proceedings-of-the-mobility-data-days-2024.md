Data provision in urban and regional areas | Regulatory frameworks and practical implications
Sabrina Ropp | Recent revisions of key regulatory frameworks on the European level (ITS Directive and its Delegated Regulations, TEN-T Regulation, Data Act) go along with increased effects on and requirements for cities and regions in terms of data provision and digitalisation. In this session, representatives from the European Commission, POLIS, EMTA, UITP and the City of Vienna will explore what these regulatory frameworks imply for cities, regions and public transport. Focus will be set on how responsibilities on different geographical and jurisdictional levels can be untangled, how regulations are implemented in practice and how awareness for the National Access Points can be raised and added value for the local level can be created.

NAPs in the European Mobility Data Space
The common European mobility data space (EMDS) aims to create a unified
framework for data sharing across various transport ecosystems and platforms in Europe. National Access Points (NAPs) are a critical component of this vision, specifically focused on making data available as required under the ITS Directive. This session will explore how NAPs contribute to the broader EMDS by providing essential data and how the EMDS interlinking layer could ensure more and more mobility data ecosystems are being interconnected to ensure smooth cross-border
exchange and use of information. By joining efforts, both NAPCORE and
Timo Hoffmann | deployEMDS aim to overcome the current fragmentation in mobility data, working towards a more interconnected and efficient European transport system, spanning the local, national and European levels. Participants will also gain insights into the strategic frameworks, technical standards, and long-term governance models that are being conceptualised to guide the EMDS and NAPs towards a more unified, interoperable future.

Strategies for NAPs to maximize MMTIS data consumption – Interactive
workshop
The revised MMTIS DR introduced data quality as a lever to make sure that data exposed on NAPs are actually used (or consumed). But, data quality has lots of different meanings. Are we referring to compliance to data standards, completeness of the data or its capacity to be used by data consumers? Are we also discussing the match between data and real-life? And, how are all of this supporting the maximization of MMTIS data consumption?
Let’s have an interactive workshop to explore how National Access Points (NAPs) can enhance the use of MMTIS data through best practices, tools, and strategies to improve data accessibility. We want to foster collaboration and drive innovation in mobility services, as all participants are facing the same questions. Let’s also learn from each other and make sure resources are not wasted re-inventing the wheel.

Meet METR: Introduction for policymakers on Management of Electronic
Traffic Regulations
Shaping the future of trusted data driven mobility is the key of this session, aimed at policy and decision makers in the realm of traffic rules and traffic regulations. Making sure that in the digital era, the end users either being human drivers or automated vehicles, have trustworthy access to the digital rules of the road (both static and dynamic) requires a standardised interoperable ecosystem of roles, responsibilities and technology. This challenge is not only European, but requires a global approach. In this session we will, together with the US DoT clarify the collaboration on the work that needs to done on global, european and local level.

Navigating NAPs: Content, Service quality and Accessibility Under the ITS Directive
The session will cover various aspects of NAPs, including the content required by the
amended ITS Directive ((EU) 2023/2661), the advertising of provided content
distributions (data catalogues), and the subscription processes, formats, and
protocols (including documentation) that ensure the content is legally and technically
accessible. It will also address monitoring the quality and accessibility of provided
content using the Integrated Quality Management System (InQMS) and visualizing
consumed content through EuroRoaSt. Additionally, the session will briefly touch on
the costs to operate NAPs and consumer pricing preferences, based on survey
results. You will learn how these elements fit together, the current reality as revealed
by the DATEX@NAPs study covering all EU countries, and the tools and best

NAP Interoperability Demonstrators
“NAPCORE is dedicated to understanding how to enable better cross-border
interoperability and ensure the continuity of ITS Services through the use of
NAPs. Looking into different options while fostering Member States’ cooperation
around topics of common interest, a specific task was designed to support those
concepts to come to life by developing the NAP interoperability demonstrators.
These demonstrators serve as tangible examples of how we can bridge gaps,
foster cross-border connectivity, and ensure the seamless flow of ITS services.
The session offers a thorough overview, including live demonstrations, outlining
the key outcomes and the main constraints and challenges encountered by each
of the four demonstrators developed as part of this task:

NAPs’ Levels of Service and Reference Architecture
“The NAP ecosystem is highly diverse across European Member States, featuring
various typologies, interfaces, and functionalities. Therefore, there is a need for
enhanced compatibility and interoperability of NAP features. WG2 of NAPCORE is
dedicated to improving interoperability and the Level of Service (LoS) of National
Access Points. This session will focus on two key WG2 initiatives aimed at advancing
NAP interoperability:
1) The NAP LoS KPI Framework, which establishes common definitions and a
harmonized process for assessing the LoS of a NAP;
2) The NAP Reference Architecture, which details both the essential and additional
architectural elements within a NAP.
The session will begin with an overview of the LoS Framework and the Reference
Architecture. Following this, we will have an interactive part, gathering multiple NAP
operators to share how they are leveraging these tools to enhance their systems at
Witsch Benjamin | the national level, and offering the audience a chance to participate in the discussion on advancing the interoperability of ITS services.”
